\begin{abstract}
Sunfish is a chess engine whose whole logic fits in 142 lines of Python,
and whose docstrings state, in unusually precise English, the contracts
its search is supposed to satisfy.  We report on a campaign that
treated those docstrings as latent formal specifications: we transcribed
them into Lean~4, proved the ones that are true, refuted the ones that
are not, named the ones that are only conditionally true, and --- where
a condition turned out to be both load-bearing and \emph{false} ---
changed the engine so that it verifies at run time what it had been
assuming.  In parallel, every resulting engine change was priced with
ELO tournaments under a fixed empirical methodology.
The formal deliverables include a sorry-free proof of the engine's core
fail-soft null-window contract; a machine-checked seven-position
counterexample showing the original stalemate correction is sound only
under a named hypothesis; a proof of a maintainer-conjectured invariant
about killer moves, later strengthened to a full lifecycle theorem; and
a machine-checked transposition-table inconsistency induced by
gamma-adaptive Late Move Reductions.  For the latter we first proved an
interval-shaped statement --- fail highs bound one value function, fail
lows another, the truth in between --- and then deleted it: such a
claim is a guarantee only if the gap between the two functions is
bounded, and no bound on search instability is provable (one ply of
depth can hide a mate), so the interval reading was never a
specification, only a description of a bug.  The only contract the
engine recognizes is the \emph{point spec}: every stored bound
describes one value function determined by the transposition key.
The campaign's second half is a single mistake found three times.
Sunfish repeatedly inferred \emph{legality} from a \emph{score}: a
fail-soft report below a sentinel was read as ``this node has no legal
moves''.  Three such inferences were refuted on real boards, each with
a witness position and a real contradictory table entry.  The repair is
architectural --- \emph{verify-on-suspicion}: the move loop carries a
cheap legality certificate alongside the value fold, and pays for a
direct board-predicate proof only when a bound would otherwise cross a
terminal value without one.  With it, the invariant that a
king-capturable node reports exactly the mate sentinel --- refuted for
the old loop --- is restored \emph{by construction} and proved for the
shipped consumer, which is in turn proved equal to an executable
reference specification.  The final premise to fall was a chess
statement (``you cannot be in zugzwang while delivering forced mate''),
which we could not assume, because a counterexample search found it to
be false; the engine now settles the question with one search probe at
a window where both failure directions are decisive.  The result is a
correctness layer --- the search's bracketing property and its table's
non-contradiction --- that carries \emph{no chess assumption at all};
zugzwang survives only in a second layer that governs accuracy and that
the table can never depend on.  Each remaining assumption is a named
hypothesis in a ledger of bets and, where possible, measured on the
production engine, which plays publicly on Lichess.  Proofs identify
the trade; tournaments price it; and when the trade is a false premise,
neither loop is enough --- the engine has to be changed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sunfish\footnote{\url{https://github.com/thomasahle/sunfish}} is a
minimalist chess engine: with comments and whitespace removed, the
engine proper is 142 lines of Python, yet it plays at ratings above 2000
on Lichess, where it runs in production as the bot
\texttt{sunfish-engine}.  What makes it an unusual verification target
is not its size but its \emph{documentation}: the docstring of its
central routine, \texttt{Searcher.bound}, is a complete two-sided
fail-soft contract, stated a decade before anyone tried to prove it.
The comments scattered through the search --- ``\texttt{lower <= s(pos)
<= upper}'', ``the opponent will for sure just stand pat'', ``sunfish
may report `mate', but then after more search realize it's not a mate
after all'' --- turn out to be, respectively: a table invariant, a
dischargeable lemma, and the exact failure mode of a hypothesis that a
machine-checked counterexample shows to be necessary.  The docstrings
were latent formal specs.

This paper reports a campaign, conducted over five days on the live
repository, that ran two loops in parallel:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The proof loop.}  Every search feature was transcribed
    into a small, dependency-free Lean~4 model (core Lean plus the
    \texttt{omega} tactic; no Mathlib; a full build takes seconds) and
    either proven sorry-free, refuted by an explicit machine-checked
    counterexample, or reduced to a \emph{named hypothesis} whose
    statement --- not whose proof --- is the deliverable.
  \item \textbf{The price loop.}  Every engine change motivated by the
    proofs (and every change the proofs advised against) was priced by
    an ELO tournament between frozen engine builds, under the
    methodology of Section~\ref{sec:empirical}, on the same engine that
    plays real games on Lichess.
\end{itemize}

The two loops answer different questions and neither substitutes for the
other.  A proof tells you \emph{what} a pruning trick costs in
soundness --- which invariant it weakens, at which positions, under
which window.  A tournament tells you what that weakening costs (or
earns) \emph{in ELO}.  Several of our measured numbers
(Table~\ref{tab:elo}) show soundness restorations that are strictly ELO-negative,
and unsound-in-the-point-sense optimizations that are strongly
ELO-positive; the formal work is what lets us merge the latter with our
eyes open, with a two-line containment for the specific inconsistency
the proof predicts --- and, once the interval reading of that
containment was recognized as guaranteeing nothing, to retire the
optimization deliberately at a measured price
(Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}) --- and finally, when a last measurement
campaign showed that the honest form of the optimization is worth
exactly zero, to delete the mechanism outright, formal apparatus and
all (Section~\ref{sec:removal}).

A third loop appeared once the campaign was under way, and it is the
one this draft is mostly about.  Some named hypotheses are not bets
worth keeping: they are \emph{false}, and load-bearing.  When that
happens neither proving nor pricing helps --- the engine has to be
changed so that it stops needing the assumption.  Three times in this
campaign a hypothesis of the form ``a score below this sentinel means
no legal move exists'' was refuted on a real board, with a real
contradictory transposition entry to show for it
(Section~\ref{sec:refuted}); the response was an architectural change,
\emph{verify-on-suspicion} (Section~\ref{sec:verify}), that replaces
the inference with a cheap certificate and a direct check.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A sorry-free Lean~4 proof that sunfish's fail-soft null-window
search satisfies its own docstring (Section~\ref{sec:contract}) --- to
our knowledge the first Lean~4 formalization of null-window/MTD-bi
search.
(2) A faithful model of the engine's king-capture convention and
stalemate correction, with a proof under four individually necessary
hypotheses and a machine-checked counterexample showing the key
hypothesis cannot be dropped (Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}).
(3) A soundness taxonomy for selective search
(Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), with futility pruning and Late Move
Reductions as the canonical citizens of its two nontrivial classes,
and the doctrine it produced: there is no interval spec.
(4) \emph{A score is not a legality proof.}  Three refuted
``score implies legality'' assumptions, each with a real board witness
and a real crossed table entry (Section~\ref{sec:refuted}); the
verify-on-suspicion architecture that removes them
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}); and the proof that the shipped consumer
computes exactly the same function as an executable reference
specification (\texttt{production\_eq\_reference}).
(5) A correctness layer with \emph{no chess premises}: the search's
bracketing property against its own declared value function, and the
non-contradiction of its transposition table, hold unconditionally ---
including the last chess statement, which was discharged by a run-time
band-edge probe after a counterexample search showed it was false
(Section~\ref{sec:bandedge}).
(6) An explicit invariant catalogue: what the engine guarantees, by
what mechanism each guarantee is earned, and how they compose into the
top-level statement, with human-readable proof sketches
(Section~\ref{sec:invariants}).
(7) The ledger-of-bets discipline (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}), the
model--code drift guard (Section~\ref{sec:drift}), and the empirical
methodology with honest numbers, including artifacts we initially
believed (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}).

\paragraph{Reading guide.}
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines, once and explicitly, the model in which
every theorem of this paper is stated --- what is a Lean definition,
what is a named hypothesis, what is not modeled at all --- so that each
later ``Theorem'' can be read with its exact scope in mind.
Section~\ref{sec:inventory} then inventories every machine-checked
result: Table~\ref{tab:theorems} lists the proved theorems with their
Lean names and files, and Table~\ref{tab:cex} lists the machine-checked
\emph{counterexamples}, which are deliverables of equal rank.
Sections~\ref{sec:contract}--\ref{sec:taxonomy} tell the story of the
early results in the order they were found;
Sections~\ref{sec:refuted}--\ref{sec:bandedge} tell the story of the
repair.  A reader who wants only the current state of the engine ---
what is guaranteed and why --- can read Section~\ref{sec:invariants}
alone; it is self-contained and is the paper's answer to ``what,
exactly, is proved about the code that ships?''

\section{The Model: What the Theorems Quantify Over}
\label{sec:model}

Every result in this paper is a theorem about a small Lean~4 development
(\texttt{formal/} in the sunfish repository: eight files, no dependencies
beyond core Lean and the \texttt{omega} tactic), not about the Python
text of \texttt{sunfish.py}.  This section fixes the model once, so that
each later theorem can be read with its exact scope in mind; how the
model--code gap is managed is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:lessons},
and Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows the correspondence at a glance.

\subsection{Abstract games, king-capture legality, and the true value}
\label{sec:model-games}

The base structure is chess-free:

\begin{definition}[Game]
\label{def:game}
A \emph{game} $G$ consists of a type of positions $\mathrm{Pos}$, a
successor function $\mathrm{moves} : \mathrm{Pos} \to
\mathrm{List\ Pos}$, and a static evaluation $\mathrm{eval} :
\mathrm{Pos} \to \mathbb{Z}$.  The empty move list means \emph{the side
to move has lost}.  \leanref{Game}{GameTree.lean}
\end{definition}

Sunfish is a \emph{king-capture} engine: it generates pseudo-legal
moves, and legality is enforced one ply late by making king capture
decisive.  The model adopts this convention wholesale: a position with
$\mathrm{eval}(p) \le -\ML$ has already lost its king, and every search
in the development normalizes such positions to the exact sentinel
$-\MU$.  ``Check'', ``mate'' and ``stalemate'' are therefore not
primitives of the model; they are emergent, exactly as in the engine
(Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}).  Features that need more structure extend
$\mathrm{Game}$ conservatively: a \emph{NullGame} adds a pass move
$\mathrm{pass} : \mathrm{Pos} \to \mathrm{Pos}$ (the model of
\texttt{pos.rotate(nullmove=True)}); a \emph{ValGame} adds per-move
values $\mathrm{val}$ together with sunfish's incremental-score identity
$\mathrm{eval}(m) = -(\mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p,m))$ as a
structural field (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}); killer- and
transposition-table models additionally assume decidable equality of
positions, for table keying.

The ``true score'' $s^*$ of the docstring is \emph{defined} to be
depth-limited negamax: $\negamax_d(p) = \mathrm{eval}(p)$ at $d = 0$,
otherwise the maximum of $-\negamax_{d-1}(m)$ over
$m \in \mathrm{moves}(p)$, folded from $\LOSS = -\MU$
\leanref{negamax}{GameTree.lean}.  Variants of the value function ---
$\negamaxD$ (draw-aware, Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}) and
$\mathrm{nullValue}$ (null-move- and
repetition-augmented, Section~\ref{sec:nullmove}) --- are likewise
definitions, not axioms.  (Two further variants have been
\emph{deleted} from the development along with the mechanisms they
specified: the $(\Vhi, \Vlo)$ pair once defined for re-search LMR ---
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} tells that story and states the doctrine
behind the deletion --- and $\mathrm{negamaxDet}$, the
per-move-reduced-depth value of the deterministic-LMR era, deleted
when all reductions were removed at commit \texttt{7fdd741},
Section~\ref{sec:removal}.)

\subsection{The searches are Lean functions, one per feature}
\label{sec:model-searches}

\texttt{Searcher.bound} is modeled as a total recursive Lean function
mirroring the Python control flow, and each search feature is modeled in
its own file as a variant of that function: the bare fail-soft skeleton
$\mathrm{bound}$ \leanref{bound}{Bound.lean}; table threading
($\mathrm{boundTT}$), futility ($\mathrm{boundFut}$) and mate-score
softening in \texttt{Tricks.lean}; the stalemate correction
($\mathrm{boundStale}$, \texttt{Stalemate.lean}); the killer table as
state ($\mathrm{boundKill}$, \texttt{Killer.lean}); and the
search with null move, repetition check, $(\mathrm{pos},
\mathrm{depth})$-keyed table and IID ($\mathrm{boundNullTT}$,
\texttt{CanNull.lean}).  Two later files complete the picture:
\texttt{EvalBounds.lean}, which discharges numeric hypotheses by
\texttt{decide} over the transcribed piece-square tables
(Section~\ref{sec:invariants}), and \texttt{Driver.lean}, which models
the MTD-bi bisection itself and proves which windows it can actually
probe (Section~\ref{sec:mtdbi}).  The two consumers of the current
design --- an executable reference specification ($\mathrm{boundD2}$)
and the shipped loop ($\mathrm{boundKCX}$) --- are both in
\texttt{Stalemate.lean}, together with the theorem that they compute
the same function (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).  Two
further models existed while the mechanisms they specified shipped ---
deterministic LMR ($\mathrm{boundLmrDet}$, \texttt{LmrDet.lean}) and
the historical re-search LMR ($\mathrm{boundLmr}$, \texttt{Lmr.lean})
--- and were deleted with those mechanisms at commit \texttt{7fdd741}
(Section~\ref{sec:removal}); git is the archive.  Studying
features in separate models is itself a modeling decision, and where
features could interact the development proved they do not: while the
reductions existed, futility (depth $\le 1$) and LMR (depth $\ge 3$)
never co-occurred (\texttt{futilityLmrDisjoint}) and reductions never
touched king captures (\texttt{RedRespectsCaptures}) --- both theorems
proven in \texttt{Lmr.lean} and now in git history with it --- which is
what let the killer and stalemate arguments survive under LMR while it
shipped.

\subsection{Nothing is axiomatized; conditions are named hypotheses}
\label{sec:model-axioms}

The development contains no \texttt{axiom} declarations and no
\texttt{sorry}.  Everything above is a Lean \emph{definition}; every
theorem is proven from those definitions, and \texttt{\#print axioms}
on the main theorems reports only \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound} (the ambient axioms of Lean's kernel).  Where a
claim about the engine is only \emph{conditionally} true, the condition
is not assumed globally: it is a \emph{named hypothesis carried in the
theorem statement} --- e.g.\ \emph{Bounded} (evaluations lie in
$[-\MU,\MU]$), \emph{CheckProbeOK}, \emph{MateValuesAreKingCaptures},
\emph{NullBetOK}, \emph{FutilityMateOK} --- and
Section~\ref{sec:ledger} maintains the full ledger.  Machine-checked
counterexamples are concrete finite games (three to seven positions)
whose search values are computed by Lean's kernel via \texttt{decide};
they are theorems of the form ``this universally quantified statement is
false'', not informal counterarguments.

\subsection{What is \emph{not} modeled}
\label{sec:model-not}

Theorems quantify over \emph{all} games of
Definition~\ref{def:game}; sunfish's chess is one intended instance, but
board representation and move generation are entirely abstract.

The repository enforces a standing rule --- \emph{the model must always
do what the code does} --- so a divergence between the two is treated
as a defect to be fixed in the same commit, not as a footnote.  What
survives that rule is a short, closed list of deliberate \emph{scoped
abstractions}: places where the model draws a layer boundary rather
than making a claim about the shipped code that is false.  The list is
maintained in \texttt{formal/README.md} and reproduced here in full;
it is the honest answer to ``what is \emph{not} verified?''.

how the search treats whatever moves it is given.  And the
model--code correspondence itself is established by audit plus an
automated drift guard (Section~\ref{sec:drift}), not by machine proof:
that bridge is the largest remaining piece of trust in the
construction, and we say so again in Section~\ref{sec:lessons}.

\section{The Core Contract}
\label{sec:contract}

\subsection{The specification, transcribed verbatim}

The docstring of \texttt{Searcher.bound} (sunfish.py, lines 286--290)
reads:

\begin{quote}\ttfamily
Let s* be the "true" score of the sub-tree we are searching.\\
The method returns r, where\\
if gamma >~ s* then s* <= r < gamma\hspace{1em}(A better upper bound)\\
if gamma <= s* then gamma <= r <= s*\hspace{0.55em}(A better lower bound)
\end{quote}

In the model of Section~\ref{sec:model}, the ``true score'' $s^*$ is the
depth-limited negamax value $\negamax_d(p)$, and the docstring
transcribes to a two-sided fail-soft condition on the report $r$,
illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:failsoft}.  Here $\MU = 69290$ and
$\ML = 50710$ are sunfish's \texttt{MATE\_UPPER} and
\texttt{MATE\_LOWER}.

\begin{definition}[BoundSpec, the docstring]
\label{def:boundspec}
For a game $G$, depth $d$, position $p$, window $\gamma$ and report $r$,
\[
  \mathrm{BoundSpec}(G,d,p,\gamma,r) \;:\equiv\;
  \bigl(\gamma \le r \rightarrow r \le \negamax_d(p)\bigr)
  \;\wedge\;
  \bigl(r < \gamma \rightarrow \negamax_d(p) \le r\bigr).
\]
\leanref{BoundSpec}{Bound.lean}
\end{definition}

The search itself, stripped to its logical skeleton, is the fail-soft
loop with the early cutoff on $\mathit{best} \ge \gamma$, over children
searched with the flipped null window $1-\gamma$ one ply shallower
\leanref{bound, searchMoves}{Bound.lean}.  No table, no null move, no
quiescence, no killer: those return in
Sections~\ref{sec:stalemate}--\ref{sec:ledger}.

\begin{theorem}[The docstring is true]
\label{thm:bound-spec}
For every game $G$, depth $d$, position $p$ and window
$\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}$,
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}\bigl(G,d,p,\gamma,\mathrm{bound}_d(p,\gamma)\bigr)$
holds.
\leanref{bound\_spec}{Bound.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on depth.  The loop is handled by a single invariant lemma
\leanref{searchMoves\_spec}{Bound.lean}: \emph{the running
$\mathit{best}$ is itself a fail-soft-correct report of the running true
maximum}.  On a cutoff, $\mathit{best} \ge \gamma$ came either from the
current child (whose report is $\le$ its true value by the induction
hypothesis) or from the previous $\mathit{best}$ (bounded by the
invariant); either way the fold over the unsearched tail can only grow.
Without a cutoff, both the child and the previous $\mathit{best}$ failed
low, so both upper-bound their true counterparts and the invariant is
re-established.  The null-window step is the integer fact
$b < 1-\gamma \iff \gamma \le -b$: a child failing low against
$1-\gamma$ fails high for the parent, and vice versa.  The proof is
sorry-free; \texttt{\#print axioms} reports only \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this is the load-bearing spec: the MTD-bi driver}
\label{sec:mtdbi}

Sunfish's driver \texttt{Searcher.search} is MTD-bi (a bisecting variant
of MTD($f$)~\cite{plaat1996mtdf}): at each depth it maintains a bracket
$\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{score} \le \mathit{upper}$, initialized to
$[1-\MU, \MU]$, and repeatedly probes \texttt{bound} at
$\gamma = \lfloor(\mathit{lower}+\mathit{upper}+1)/2\rfloor$, stopping
when the bracket is narrower than \texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS} ($=15$
centipawns) --- Figure~\ref{fig:mtdbi}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec} is exactly the property the binary search
needs: \emph{every} probe, whatever its $\gamma$, moves one end of the
bracket --- a fail high raises $\mathit{lower}$ to $r$, a fail low
lowers $\mathit{upper}$ to $r$, and the two can never cross
(Theorem~\ref{thm:no-crossing} below makes the no-crossing claim
formal, and Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} shows how the since-removed
re-search Late Move Reductions broke precisely it).  Fail-softness is
what makes bisection
converge fast in practice: the report $r$ typically overshoots
$\gamma$, so the bracket shrinks by more than half.

Two details of the driver are load-bearing for later sections.  The
first is that the bracket invariant is exactly what the LMR crossing
counterexample (Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}) broke --- and, because the
spread of the crossing reports admits no provable bound, there was no
slack (not \texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS}, not re-probing) that could
honestly be said to absorb it (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}) --- the fact
that eventually killed the mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:removal}).

The second is the \emph{window range}, and it is a small cautionary
tale about believing a comment.  Almost every theorem downstream ---
the stalemate correction, the killer invariant, the current layered
specs --- carries a hypothesis of the form ``$\gamma$ is in band''.
That hypothesis is discharged only if the driver never probes outside
the band, and for a long time both this paper's source and the engine's
own comment asserted that MTD-bi only probes $(-\ML, \ML]$.  Modeling
the driver (\texttt{Driver.lean}) showed the assertion is
\emph{true for every window computed at the current depth while scores
stay strictly inside the band} (\texttt{driver\_band\_invariant}) and
\emph{false} for the first probe of a depth after a mate-band score:
sunfish carries $\gamma$ across the per-depth bracket reset, and a
forced-mate score carries it above the band
(\texttt{carried\_gamma\_escapes\_band}, machine-checked).

There were two ways to close the gap: clamp $\gamma$, or widen the
bracket to an interval that already contains everything the driver can
produce.  The engine shipped the clamp first, then replaced it: the
bracket is now the full flip-closed band $[1-\MU, \MU]$ and the clamp
is deleted.  The payoff is unconditional ---
\texttt{driver\_wide\_is\_now\_the\_range} and
\texttt{dstep\_wide\_sides} prove that \emph{every} probe window,
carried ones included, lies in $(1-\MU, \MU]$, with no clamp and no
assumption about scores --- so the in-band hypothesis is discharged
once, at the driver, for every theorem that consumes it.  Widening also
fixed a latent bug the model surfaced in passing: a fail-soft mate
score exceeds any narrower upper end, which inverts the bracket.  The
cost is nil, because fail-softness means the first probe collapses the
bracket wherever it started.

\section{The King-Capture Model and the Stalemate Correction}
\label{sec:stalemate}

Sunfish generates \emph{pseudo-legal} moves and scores an actual king
capture as an immediate win: a position whose static score is
$\le -\ML$ has already lost its king and is normalized to exactly
$-\MU$.  ``No legal moves'' (mate \emph{or} stalemate) therefore
surfaces in the search as $\mathit{best} = -\MU$ after the loop, and the
engine post-processes it (at $\mathrm{depth} > 2$) with an in-check
probe on the null-moved position:
$\mathit{best} := -\ML$ if in check, else $0$.

\begin{definition}[Draw-aware value]
$\negamaxD_d(p)$ is negamax over the king-capture-normalized game with
the depth-gated stalemate correction applied to the fold: if
$d > 2$ and the fold equals $\LOSS$, the value becomes $-\ML$ if $p$ is
in check and $0$ otherwise. \leanref{negamaxDraw, drawFix}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CheckProbeOK]
A probe function agrees pointwise with the one-ply king-capture reading
of check: after passing the turn, the opponent has a move reaching a
position with $\mathrm{eval} \le -\ML$.  For the real engine this is the
assumption that the depth-0 QS probe
\texttt{bound(flipped, MATE\_UPPER, 0) == MATE\_UPPER} decides check ---
true because quiescence never prunes king captures.
\leanref{CheckProbeOK, inCheckB}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[MateValuesAreKingCaptures]
\label{hyp:mvakc}
Whenever $\negamaxD_d(p) = \MU$ with $d \ge 1$, some move of $p$
actually reaches a position with $\mathrm{eval} \le -\ML$: mate-band
sentinel values always come from a real king capture, never from a
shallow-horizon artifact.
\leanref{MateValuesAreKingCaptures}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[The corrected search satisfies the docstring]
\label{thm:boundstale}
Let $G$ be a game with a pass move, and suppose:
(i) all evaluations lie in $[-\MU, \MU]$ (\emph{Bounded});
(ii) the probe satisfies \emph{CheckProbeOK};
(iii) the window is in band: $-\MU < \gamma \le \MU$ (an interval closed
under the null-window flip $\gamma \mapsto 1-\gamma$); and
(iv) \emph{MateValuesAreKingCaptures}.
Then the stalemate-corrected search $\mathrm{boundStale}$ satisfies
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ against $\negamaxD$ at every depth and position.
\leanref{boundStale\_spec}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The same loop invariant as Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}, plus a
pointwise lemma \leanref{staleFix\_spec\_core}{Stalemate.lean} showing
the correction maps a fail-soft-correct pair (search $\mathit{best}$,
true fold) to a fail-soft-correct pair.  The crux is the masking step:
if the \emph{true} fold is $\LOSS$, then (by the band, the sentinel
invariant, and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:mvakc}) every child is an exact
king-capture mate, every child report is exactly $\LOSS$, and hence the
search's $\mathit{best}$ is $\LOSS$ too --- so both sides apply the
\emph{same} correction, using the correct probe.  In-band windows are
needed so that a fail-low at $\LOSS$ certifies the loop ran to
completion: a $\gamma \le -\MU$ would allow an early cutoff at
$\LOSS$ with unsearched moves remaining.  Sorry-free.
\end{proof}

Each hypothesis is individually necessary; the interesting one is (iv):

\begin{counterexample}[The correction is not unconditionally sound]
\label{cex:stale}
There is a seven-position game with bounded honest evaluations, a
perfectly correct probe, and an in-band window $\gamma = -5$, on which
the faithful depth-gated correction returns a fail-low ``upper bound''
of $-20$ while the draw-aware value is $0$.  The mechanism: two
positions are terminal stalemates; seen at remaining depth $\le 2$ (gate
off) they score $\LOSS$, so their parents at depth 2 score a fabricated
$\MU$ --- mate scores with no king capture anywhere behind them --- and
the root inherits a wrong bound.  This is sunfish's own code comment
(``sunfish may report `mate', but then after more search realize it's
not a mate after all'') turned into a machine-checked refutation.
\leanref{boundStale\_not\_unconditional, Cex, cex\_violates\_hypothesis}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{counterexample}

\begin{theorem}[Depth inconsistency is intrinsic]
\label{thm:depth-inconsistent}
The $\mathrm{depth} > 2$ gate makes the draw-aware value itself
depth-dependent: in the counterexample game there is a stalemate
position $g$ with $\negamaxD_2(g) = \LOSS$ and $\negamaxD_3(g) = 0$.
No depth-independent game value can agree with $\negamaxD$ at every
depth.
\leanref{negamaxDraw\_depth\_inconsistent}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Why mate is $-\ML$, not $-\MU$]
$-\MU$ is the reserved sentinel meaning ``the king is \emph{actually}
capturable/gone''.  Scoring checkmate-by-correction as $-\MU$ would
re-inject the sentinel at positions whose king is not capturable and
destroy the parent-level detection one ply up; it would also order
``mated next move'' above ``king already lost''.  The engine's choice of
$-\ML$ in the correction is what keeps the sentinel trustworthy, and
the distinction is load-bearing for everything in
Sections~\ref{sec:refuted}--\ref{sec:invariants}: $-\MU$ is a
\emph{claim about the board}, $-\ML$ is a claim about the game.  The
campaign's central bug is what happens when the two are conflated.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The margin was almost too small]
\label{rem:margin}
Modeling the numbers rather than the algorithm produced one shipped
repair.  For the sentinel argument to work, the mate band must be wide
enough that no static evaluation can reach it; the engine originally
set $\ML = K - 10Q$.  Checking that by \texttt{decide} over the
transcribed piece-square tables found a leak
(\texttt{kingGone\_check\_leaked}): a position with the king gone
\emph{and} enough queens could evaluate above $-\ML$, so the ``have we
still got a king?'' test would miss.  The repair, $\ML = K - 13Q$
(now $47{,}923$), is proven to close it (\texttt{margin\_covers}), and
the general fact --- no static evaluation reaches the band --- is
\texttt{evalBound\_lt\_MATE\_LOWER}, which is what discharges
\emph{Bounded} for the shipped tables rather than assuming it.
\end{remark}

\section{The Killer Invariant: Conjectured at Noon, Proved by Evening}
\label{sec:killer}

While modeling the stalemate correction we recorded a potential gap: the
killer move (the stored \texttt{tp\_move} entry) is yielded \emph{before}
the sorted moves, so a non-capture killer failing high could let
\texttt{bound} return a value below $\MU$ at a king-capturable position
--- breaking the sentinel requirement that
Theorem~\ref{thm:boundstale} leans on.  The maintainer conjectured this
cannot happen in sunfish.  The conjecture was proved the same day.

\begin{definition}[KillerOK, the table invariant]
A killer table $t$ (a partial map from positions to moves) satisfies
$\mathrm{KillerOK}$ if every stored entry is a legal move of its key
position, and at a king-capturable position the stored entry --- if any
--- is \emph{itself a king capture}.
\leanref{KillerOK}{Killer.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[KillerIsKingCapture]
\label{thm:killer}
Thread \texttt{tp\_move} through the search as state (position-keyed,
store-on-fail-high-only), with the move loop running over
value-ordered moves (king captures, value $\ge \ML$, strictly first).
Then for every depth $d$, position $p$, in-band window
$-\MU < \gamma \le \MU$, and table $t$ satisfying $\mathrm{KillerOK}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the call $\mathrm{boundKill}_d(p, \gamma, t)$ returns a table
    satisfying $\mathrm{KillerOK}$; and
  \item if $d \ge 1$, $p$'s own king is not already gone, and $p$ is
    king-capturable, the returned value is \emph{exactly} $\MU$ --- on
    the killer path and on the loop path alike.
\end{enumerate}
\leanref{boundKill\_spec}{Killer.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on depth, cases on the table lookup.  \emph{Killer-cutoff
case}: the stored move is the killer itself; by the invariant it is a
king capture whenever $p$ is capturable, the king-capture normalization
scores it at the full sentinel, and an in-band $\gamma$ makes the
sentinel fail high --- so the cutoff both stores a king capture and
reports exactly $\MU$.  \emph{Killer-fail-low case}: a king-capture
killer would have reported $\MU \ge \gamma$, so $p$ is not capturable
and plain loop preservation applies.  \emph{No-killer case}: at a
capturable position the capture heads the ordered list, cuts off
immediately at the sentinel, and nothing else can be stored first
\leanref{killLoop\_capture\_head}{Killer.lean}.  Applied along any
execution history starting from the empty table
\leanref{killerEmpty\_OK}{Killer.lean}, the invariant holds forever.
Sorry-free; axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} only.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact sentinel; fail-low certifies non-capturability]
At any king-capturable node (depth $\ge 1$, in-band window) the search
reports exactly $\MU$; contrapositively, any in-band report
${} < \MU$ --- in particular any fail low --- certifies that no king
capture was available.  This is what makes the depth-0 stalemate probe
of Section~\ref{sec:stalemate} meaningful.
\end{corollary}

Three conditions are load-bearing, and each is an edit a well-meaning PR
could make:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Position-exact keying.}  Every store into
    $\texttt{tp\_move}[p]$ happens inside a call at $p$ itself; a
    ply-shared killer table (a common engine idiom) would let a quiet
    killer harvested at one position cut off at a different,
    king-capturable one, breaking the induction.
  \item \textbf{Captures sort first.}  King captures have
    $\mathrm{value} \ge \ML$, strictly above every quiet move.
  \item \textbf{In-band windows.}  An out-of-band $\gamma > \MU$ could
    let a quiet move cut off (and be stored) above the capture's
    sentinel.
\end{itemize}

The residual exception is the null-move yield: it stores nothing (so
$\mathrm{KillerOK}$ survives) but can end the loop below the sentinel;
sunfish guards it only by $|\mathrm{pos.score}| < 500$, a guard its own
comment concedes is heuristic.  The condition under which the guard
closes the hole is the named hypothesis
\leanref{NullGuardBlocksAtCaptures}{Killer.lean}.  Closing it
\emph{properly} --- by validating the yield instead of guarding it ---
is what Section~\ref{sec:verify} does.

\paragraph{Empirical companion.}  Before the proof existed, the
conjecture was checked empirically on the production engine:
\textbf{0 violations in 694{,}533 killer cutoffs over 1{,}270 real-game
positions}.  The proof upgraded ``empirically absent'' to ``impossible
given in-band windows and value ordering'' --- and, usefully, told us
exactly which future edits would void the guarantee.

\subsection{From invariant to lifecycle}
\label{sec:killerlife}

Theorem~\ref{thm:killer} is a statement about \emph{one call} from an
invariant-satisfying table.  That is the right shape for an induction
but the wrong shape for a guarantee about a running engine, which
stores from several places, evicts under memory pressure, and ---
because \texttt{tp\_move} is deliberately \emph{not} cleared between
searches --- carries entries across move boundaries.  Every one of
those is an opportunity to store something the invariant forbids.

The current development therefore proves the stronger statement
directly, over the store trace:

\begin{theorem}[The killer table's whole lifecycle]
\label{thm:killerlife}
Let $\mathrm{KillerInv}$ be the position-intrinsic invariant ``every
stored entry is a legal move of its key, and at a king-capturable key
it is itself a king capture''.  Then for \emph{any} finite sequence of
store events drawn from the engine's three store species --- the
fail-high store of a searched real winner, the substitution store of a
verified king capture, and the mate-case futility store --- interleaved
with FIFO evictions, folding the sequence from the empty table yields a
table satisfying $\mathrm{KillerInv}$; hence $\mathrm{KillerLegal}$
holds of the resulting lookup function.
\leanref{killerLegal\_lifecycle}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction over the event list; the base case is the empty table, and
each step is discharged by the species' own certificate.  A fail-high
store is justified by \texttt{storedMoveLegal}: a real report at or
above an in-band $\gamma$ can only have come from actually searching
that move, so the move is legal --- and legality is a property of the
\emph{position}, not of the search that discovered it, which is
precisely why the entry cannot go stale when reused at a later depth or
in a later search.  The substitution store writes a move that the
consumer has just verified to be a king capture
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}).  The mate-case futility store writes a move
whose value reaches the mate band, and \texttt{HighValIsKingCapture}
turns that into a king capture.  Eviction only deletes, and a
position-intrinsic invariant is preserved by deletion.  Nothing in
$\mathrm{KillerInv}$ mentions search state --- no depth, no window, no
node counter --- which is exactly why the invariant survives across
searches, and exactly why exact-position keying is load-bearing.
\end{proof}

The practical yield is a rule with teeth: \textbf{a pull request that
adds a store site must name its event species and supply that species'
certificate}, and one that ply-shares the table --- a common engine
idiom --- loses the induction outright.  The upgrade also changes the
status of \emph{KillerLegal} everywhere downstream: it is no longer a
premise of the shipped design's specs but a theorem they may cite
(Table~\ref{tab:theorems}).

\section{A Soundness Taxonomy for Selective Search}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

The campaign's conceptual yield is a classification of how a selective
search may consult its window $\gamma$.  Sunfish's maintainer stated it
as a principle, which we quote verbatim:

\begin{quote}
``$\gamma$ may shape termination, and may trigger shortcuts whose value
provably one-side-bounds the same function; it must never select between
incomparable evaluations.''
\end{quote}

The three classes, each with its canonical citizen:

\subsection{Class (a): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-shaped
termination --- always sound}

The beta cutoff itself: $\gamma$ decides only \emph{when the loop
stops}, and fail-soft reporting keeps every report a bound on the same
$\negamax$.  This is Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}; nothing further is
needed.  Null-window search, iterative deepening, and aspiration
re-probing all live here.

\subsection{Class (b): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-triggered
shortcuts that one-side-bound the same function --- sound}

Futility pruning is the canonical citizen.  At $\mathrm{depth} \le 1$,
once $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m) < \gamma$, sunfish answers
with the \emph{static estimate} $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m)$
instead of searching the child.  The estimate is below $\gamma$, so it
is only ever a fail-low report, and $\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ demands of a
fail-low report exactly one inequality:
$-\negamax_d(m) \le \mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m)$.

We initially stated that inequality as a hypothesis
(\emph{FutilityOK}).  It was then \emph{discharged}: sunfish builds
child positions incrementally, and the score identity
\[
  \mathrm{eval}(m) \;=\; -\bigl(\mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p, m)\bigr)
  \qquad (m \in \mathrm{moves}(p))
\]
is literal in \texttt{Position.move}.  With the identity as a
structural property of the model \leanref{ValGame}{Tricks.lean}, the
futility test $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val} < \gamma$ is
integer-equivalent to ``the child's stand-pat meets its window
$1-\gamma$'', at depth 0 the stand-pat is yielded first and fails high
immediately, and fail-soft returns exactly the child's score:

\begin{theorem}[FutilityOK discharged --- as an equality]
\label{thm:futility}
In any game with the score identity, for every position $p$ and move
$m \in \mathrm{moves}(p)$,
\[
  -\negamax_0(m) \;=\; \mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p, m).
\]
The futility yield is not an estimate: it \emph{equals} the depth-0
child search it replaces.
\leanref{futilityOK\_discharged}{Tricks.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Futility-pruned search satisfies BoundSpec]
\label{thm:boundfut}
For any game with the score identity, under in-band windows and the
remaining hypothesis \emph{FutilityMateOK} (a move valued as a king
capture, $\mathrm{val} \ge \ML$, really wins: the fail-high
\texttt{else MATE\_UPPER} bypass), the futility-pruned search satisfies
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ against plain $\negamax$ --- a point spec on a
single value function.
\leanref{boundFut\_spec}{Tricks.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At depth 1 (the futility zone) each per-move yield is analyzed by cases:
a futile quiet move yields exactly $-\mathrm{eval}(m)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:futility}; a futile king capture yields the exact
sentinel, a fail high justified by \emph{FutilityMateOK} and the in-band
window; a non-futile move is an exact depth-0 child.  A
member-restricted variant of the loop lemma
\leanref{searchMoves\_spec\_mem}{Tricks.lean} (the score identity only
speaks about legal moves) closes the loop; deeper depths are
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}'s argument verbatim.  Sorry-free.
\end{proof}

Fine print made explicit by the model: the \emph{quantified} FutilityOK
(over all depths) is not dischargeable --- plain $\negamax$ at
$d \ge 1$ has no stand-pat option --- but the search only ever consumes
the $d = 0$ instance, and that instance is the score identity on the
nose.  The original statement over-required.

\subsection{Class (c): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-selected
incomparable evaluations --- unsound in the point sense}

\emph{Re-search} Late Move Reductions --- the gamma-adaptive variant
that shipped between commits \texttt{58883ea} and \texttt{7f9f164}, and
whose formal record was kept in \texttt{Lmr.lean} until mechanism and
model were deleted together at \texttt{7fdd741}
(Section~\ref{sec:removal}; git history) --- is the canonical
citizen.  A late quiet move at $\mathrm{depth} \ge 3$ is probed at
$\mathrm{depth}-2$; a reduced fail low is yielded as-is, a reduced fail
high is re-searched at full depth and only that result is yielded.  Here $\gamma$ \emph{selects which of
two incomparable value functions the yield is a fact about}, and the
docstring becomes unprovable as stated: no single value function backs
both sides.

Our first reaction was to retreat to an interval-shaped statement.
Define the mutually recursive pair $(\Vhi, \Vlo)$ with side
alternation:
\begin{align*}
  \Vhi_d(p) &= \max_{m}\; -\Vlo_{d-1}(m)
    && \text{(all full depth)}\\
  \Vlo_d(p) &= \max_{m}\;
    \begin{cases}
      \min\bigl(-\Vhi_{d-2}(m),\, -\Vhi_{d-1}(m)\bigr)
        & \text{if $m$ reducible}\\
      -\Vhi_{d-1}(m) & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
over an abstract reducibility predicate
$\mathrm{red}(d, i, m)$ generalizing sunfish's
\texttt{depth >= 3 and i\_m >= 5 and val < QS}.  We proved,
machine-checked and sorry-free at the time (as \texttt{boundLmr\_spec}
and \texttt{lmrVal\_sandwich}), that every fail-high report of the LMR
search is $\le \Vhi_d(p)$, every fail-low report is $\ge \Vlo_d(p)$,
and $\Vlo_d(p) \le \negamax_d(p) \le \Vhi_d(p)$ pointwise --- and, for
a while, we presented this pair of statements as ``the honest
replacement'' for the docstring: an \emph{interval spec}, with the
truth trapped in between.

The verdict must be stated as plainly as the retreat was: \textbf{it
was never a spec}, and the statements --- together with the
$(\Vhi,\Vlo)$ definition itself --- have been \emph{deleted} from the
development.  A claim of the form ``fail highs bound $\Vhi$, fail lows
bound $\Vlo$, and the truth lies in between'' is a guarantee only if
the gap $\Vhi - \Vlo$ is bounded.  No bound on search instability is
provable: one ply of depth can hide a mate, so the gap is unbounded
precisely in the positions that decide games.  A guarantee relative to
an uncontrollable gap guarantees nothing; the interval reading was only
a description of the bug --- in the maintainer's phrase, a ``cope
spec''.  The only contract the repository recognizes is the
\emph{point} spec: every stored bound describes one value function
determined by the transposition key.  What \texttt{Lmr.lean} kept,
until the mechanism itself was removed and the file deleted with it
(Section~\ref{sec:removal}), was exactly what supported that doctrine:
the re-search definition itself (\texttt{boundLmr}) and the
machine-checked counterexample below.

Two facts about modeling the since-deleted statements were
\emph{surprises} --- both were claims the authors believed and the
proof assistant refused; we recount them honestly in
Section~\ref{sec:lessons}.

The practical consequence is a transposition-table pathology:

\begin{theorem}[No crossing without LMR]
\label{thm:no-crossing}
For the unreduced search, a fail-high report can never exceed a fail-low
report at the same $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth})$: both bracket
$\negamax_d(p)$, so a \texttt{tp\_score} entry always satisfies
$\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{upper}$.
(\texttt{bound\_no\_crossing}, proved in \texttt{Lmr.lean} and deleted
with the file at \texttt{7fdd741}: for the now-shipped full-width
search the fact is immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec} ---
both reports bracket the same $\negamax_d(p)$.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{counterexample}[TT crossing under LMR, machine-checked]
\label{cex:crossing}
There is a three-position game and windows $\gamma = -60, 0$ on which
the LMR search at the same $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth})$ produces a
fail-high report of $+10$ and a fail-low report of $-50$: a
\texttt{tp\_score} entry with $\mathit{lower} = 10 > -50 =
\mathit{upper}$.  The single root move looks bad shallow but is good
deep; probing $\gamma = 0$ takes the reduced branch and fails low at
$-50$, probing $\gamma = -60$ bypasses it, re-searches at full depth and
fails high at $+10$.
(\texttt{lmr\_tt\_crossing}, \texttt{Lmr.lean}; deleted with the
mechanism at \texttt{7fdd741} --- in git history at
\texttt{58883ea..7fdd741}.)
\end{counterexample}

MTD-bi's bracket $\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{score} \le \mathit{upper}$
is therefore simply broken under re-search LMR: the gap between the two
value functions admits no provable bound, so there is no theorem of the
form ``the crossing stays within what \texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS} and
re-probing absorb'' --- in the positions that decide games, it need
not.  On the production engine the crossing is real but rare:
\textbf{0.016\% of nodes} measured over real games.  The two-line
store clamp shipped at the time --- on store, widen the stale side of
the entry instead of asserting a contradiction --- measured
ELO-neutral at $+12 \pm 28$; but it was containment, not repair: it
kept crossing reports from being \emph{stored} (proved at the time as
\texttt{clamp\_no\_crossing}) while the search went on consuming them.
The \texttt{IntervalTableOK} invariant that once dressed the clamp up
as preserving a guarantee was deleted along with the interval reading
--- an invariant stated relative to an unboundable gap certifies
nothing --- and the clamp model (\texttt{TableClamp.lean}) has since
been deleted from the development entirely; git is the archive.
Killer and
stalemate lemmas were unaffected while the mechanism shipped: king
captures have
$\mathrm{val} \ge \ML > \mathit{QS} = 40$ and were never reducible
(\texttt{RedRespectsCaptures}, deleted with \texttt{Lmr.lean}); the
killer is pre-loop and
structurally never reduced; reduced fail lows are $< \gamma$ and never
reach the \texttt{tp\_move} store; and the $-\MU$ king-loss sentinel is
depth-independent, so stalemate detection still saw exact sentinels
from reduced searches.

\subsection{First epilogue: deterministic LMR restores the point
spec}
\label{sec:lmrdet}

The story seemed, for a day, to have its resolution.  At commit
\texttt{7f9f164}, master
retired the re-search variant and shipped \emph{deterministic} LMR: the
reduction depended only on $(\mathrm{depth}, \mathrm{index},
\mathrm{val})$ --- \texttt{LMR = int(depth >= 4 and i\_m >= 8 and val <
0)} --- and each move was searched \emph{once}, at
$\mathrm{depth} - 1 - \mathrm{LMR}$.  Because $\gamma$ no longer
selected
between evaluations, the search was back in class (b$'$): it computed
exact fail-soft bounds on a \emph{single} value function
$\mathrm{negamaxDet}$ (reducible moves valued one ply shallower,
recursively), with a point spec (\texttt{boundLmrDet\_spec},
\texttt{LmrDet.lean} --- proven at the time, deleted with the
mechanism, Section~\ref{sec:removal}); fail-high reports could never
exceed fail-low reports at the same key, so transposition entries were
contradiction-free by construction
(\texttt{boundLmrDet\_no\_crossing}); and the store clamp of
Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}'s containment became a provable no-op
(\texttt{clamp\_noop\_high/low}, proven at the time) and was removed
from the engine --- after which the no-op theorems and the whole clamp
model (\texttt{TableClamp.lean}) were themselves deleted from the
development, under the maintainer's principle that git is the archive
and the code carries no historical artifacts.  The
price was believed measured, not guessed: the gamma-adaptive re-search
variant appeared
about 16 ELO stronger ($-16 \pm 38$ direct; both large wins over no
LMR, or so the ledger then read --- Table~\ref{tab:elo}).  That trade
--- 16 ELO for end-to-end
point specs on single value functions --- was made deliberately.  Note
that $\mathrm{negamaxDet} \ne \negamax$: deterministic
LMR was honest about \emph{which} value function it computed bounds on,
rather than unsound about the original one.  It was also, as the next
subsection shows, mispriced --- in both directions at once.

\subsection{Second epilogue: the measurement campaign removes the
mechanism entirely}
\label{sec:removal}

The resolution of Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet} did not survive its next
measurement campaign (all matches per \texttt{docs/TESTING.md}: frozen
snapshots, paired openings, per-engine time-loss accounting).  Three
results, in the order they dismantled the trade:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Removing deterministic LMR is a gain, not a price.}
    No-LMR beat the shipped deterministic LMR by $+69 \pm 40$ and
    $+29 \pm 33$ (two 300-game matches at 4+0.04) and by $+19 \pm 45$
    at 60+1 (200 games) --- stronger at every time control tested,
    with fixed-depth suites corroborating (mate-in-4, Bratko--Kopec
    and Win-at-Chess all improve without LMR).  Deterministic LMR's
    believed $-16 \pm 38$ price against re-search was really
    ${\sim}{-50}$: net negative against \emph{no} LMR at all.  The
    methodological lesson went into \texttt{docs/TESTING.md}: a
    decision that hinges on a difference smaller than its error bars
    needs SPRT or a larger sample.
  \item \textbf{The historical ledger was never wrong.}  Replaying the
    exact A/B that merged re-search LMR at \texttt{58883ea} ($+49 \pm
    34$ then) reproduces today: $+41 \pm 36$.
  \item \textbf{The edge was the bug.}  Re-search LMR grafted onto
    today's baseline still measures $+20 \pm 36$ over no-LMR --- but
    an \emph{honest} variant with identical cutoffs and sound
    min-semantics bound propagation (each stored bound a claim about
    the minimum over the depths actually searched, key-determined by
    construction) scores exactly $0.00 \pm 34$ against no-LMR.  The
    entire measured value of re-search LMR lived in its over-claimed
    bounds: fail highs propagated as facts about a depth they did not
    search.  That is not a trade-off between strength and provability;
    it is strength borrowed from a bug --- the very transposition-table
    contradiction machine-checked in Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing} while the
    mechanism existed.
\end{itemize}

Commit \texttt{7fdd741} therefore removes Late Move Reductions
entirely.  The move loop is again a single full-width \texttt{for val,
move in sorted(...)}, every move searched at $\mathrm{depth} - 1$; with
no reduction anywhere, \texttt{bound}'s docstring is provable exactly
as written --- Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}, the point spec of
\texttt{Bound.lean}, now describes the shipped loop with no caveats
--- and the formal record needs no LMR machinery: \texttt{Lmr.lean},
\texttt{LmrDet.lean} and \texttt{TableClamp.lean} were deleted with
the mechanism, under the same git-is-the-archive principle that had
already claimed the interval spec.  What survives in the development
is what the episode taught, stated once in \texttt{formal/README.md}:
every stored bound must describe one value function determined by the
transposition key; ``the truth lies within an unboundable gap'' is not
a specification.

\section{A Score Is Not a Legality Proof}
\label{sec:refuted}

The first half of this campaign found bugs in a \emph{mechanism} ---
reductions --- and removed it.  The second half found a bug in a
\emph{habit of reasoning}, present in three different places, and
removing it required changing how the search justifies its terminal
decisions.  This section states the habit and exhibits the three
refutations; Section~\ref{sec:verify} is the repair.

Sunfish detects mate and stalemate the way most king-capture engines
do.  Because legality is enforced one ply late, ``no legal move'' is
not directly observable; instead the move loop folds per-move scores
into a running $\mathit{best}$, initialized to the fold's identity
element $\LOSS = -\MU$, and after the loop the engine reasons:

\begin{quote}
\emph{$\mathit{best}$ is still $\LOSS$, so no move improved on ``our
king is captured''; so every generated move loses the king; so there
are no legal moves; so this node is mate or stalemate and I may
overwrite the fold with an exact terminal value.}
\end{quote}

Every step of that chain is a \emph{score} being read as a statement
about \emph{legality}.  It is sound only if two things hold: every
yield that can raise the fold comes from actually searching a real
move, and a searched real move reports above the sentinel exactly when
it is legal.  In an engine with selective search, neither holds.  The
move loop also emits \emph{virtual} options --- the null move, the
quiescence stand-pat, futility estimates --- which are evidence about
\emph{value} and carry no information whatsoever about whether the side
to move has a legal move.  A virtual option that outscores the sentinel
silently converts ``this node is terminal'' into ``this node is not
terminal''.

\subsection{A1: a sound null-move yield masks a real stalemate}
\label{sec:a1}

The first instance is a fail-\emph{low} masking.  At a genuine
stalemate, the null-move option is searched and returns some value
above $-\MU$ --- soundly, since passing really is available to the
model of the position the null move creates.  That yield raises
$\mathit{best}$ off the sentinel.  The correction's guard
($\mathit{best} = -\MU$) is therefore false, the correction does not
fire, and the node returns a fail-low report as an upper bound on a
value that is really $0$.  The report is wrong in the direction that
matters: it is an \emph{upper} bound below the truth.

This is machine-checked as \texttt{a1\_unfixed\_not\_sound}, and the
statement is deliberately unflattering to the old design: the
counterexample satisfies \emph{every} hypothesis then on the books,
including the null-move bet.  The bug is not zugzwang.  It is a
category error in the fold.

The repair modeled at the time separated the accumulators: a
\texttt{best\_real} that only searched real moves may raise, tested by
the correction, alongside the null-inclusive $\mathit{best}$ that
determines the return value (\texttt{boundA1}, with
\texttt{a1\_fix\_repairs} confirming the exact $0$).  It shipped, and
it was not enough --- because the dual exists.

\subsection{The fail-high dual, and three assumptions refuted on real boards}

If a virtual option can hide a terminal node by scoring \emph{above}
the sentinel, it can also hide one by scoring above the terminal
\emph{value}.  The correction is an override: it \emph{assigns}
$\mathit{best} := 0$ (or $-\ML$ if in check) after the loop.  A
pseudo-option that scored, say, $+11$ at a position whose true value is
$0$ does not merely mask the correction --- it produces a fail-high
report of $+11$ at a node whose exact value is $0$, and a fail-low
probe of the same node stores $0$.  That is a crossed transposition
entry: $\mathit{lower} = 11 > 0 = \mathit{upper}$.

We isolated this as a named obligation, \texttt{TerminalPseudoSafe},
and machine-checked that it does not come for free
(\texttt{cexT\_crossing}).  Its instances then turned out to be not
merely unproven but \emph{false on real boards}.  Each was hunted with
a search script over real positions rather than argued about; each
produced a witness and a genuine contradictory entry from the shipped
engine of the day.

\section{Verify-on-Suspicion}
\label{sec:verify}

The repair is not a patch to the correction's guard; it is a change to
what the move loop \emph{records}.  Three ideas, in the order they
matter.

\paragraph{1. Yields have species, and the species is load-bearing.}
The engine's move generator emits five kinds of yield, and they divide
into two groups.  \emph{Real}: a searched move's result, and the
mate-case futility yield (which is a genuine king capture handed out at
the sentinel rather than pruned).  \emph{Virtual}: the null move, the
quiescence stand-pat, and sub-mate futility estimates.  Real yields are
evidence about both value and legality; virtual yields are evidence
about value only.  In the shipped code a virtual yield is literally
tagged --- it is emitted with \texttt{None} in the move slot --- and in
the model the distinction is structural (\texttt{futileAt},
\texttt{searchedAt}, \texttt{futTerm} keep synthetic suffix estimates
out of the real accumulator).

This was documentation before it was a mechanism, and the gap between
those two states cost a real bug: sub-mate futility estimates used to
be emitted as real yields, which produced the crossed entry
\texttt{Entry(lower=0, upper=-1054)}.  Making them virtual fixed it.

\paragraph{2. The fold carries a certificate.}
Alongside the value accumulator the loop maintains one boolean,
\texttt{live}: \emph{some real move has been proven legal}.  A searched
real move's score is two-way evidence --- exactly $-\MU$ proves the
move left our own king capturable, anything above proves it legal ---
so the certificate costs one comparison per yield and no searching at
all.  In the model this is the test $S = \LOSS$ on the real-only
accumulator, and \texttt{searched\_yield\_two\_way} is the lemma that
the two readings agree.

\paragraph{3. Verify only where a bound would otherwise be unjustified.}
Two places can produce an unjustified bound, and each gets a check
sized to what it needs.

\emph{The consumer interception.}  Before a \emph{virtual} yield is
allowed to end the loop, it is validated.  If a real king capture
exists, it is \emph{substituted}: the node reports $\MU$ through an
ordinary real cutoff and \texttt{tp\_move} stores the true capture ---
active preservation of the killer invariant, not merely
non-destruction.  A mate-band claim with no capture behind it is
vacuous (if passing wins the king, capturing it is a real move too) and
is folded to the identity.  A positive claim at a node already verified
terminal would outscore the exact draw and is likewise folded away.
The mate-band and terminal arms are depth-gated and the substitution
arm is not, and that asymmetry is not cosmetic: folding a terminal
stand-pat at depth 0 makes a quiescence node \emph{return} the reserved
sentinel, which we measured as 96 mismatches before the gate was
placed correctly.

\emph{The correction.}  If the node failed low and no real move was
proven legal, it is \emph{suspect} --- either it is terminal, or a
virtual option outbid every legal move --- and only then does the
engine pay for a direct proof: probe every generated move and ask
whether the reply can capture our king.

Two details of that scan matter more than they look.  It runs over the
\emph{full} generated list, not the quiescence-filtered one.  We
proposed the filtered version as an optimization, and it is unsound:
a perfectly legal child whose own legal moves all hide below its
quiescence threshold returns exactly $-\MU$ as its filtered value, so
reading scores instead of re-deriving legality reintroduces the very
inference this section exists to remove.  That is
\texttt{reducedScan\_needs\_premise}, and it refuted our own proposal
after it had been written.  And the check is a \emph{board predicate}
--- ``does this reply capture a king?'' --- not a search probe.  The
resulting doctrine is a one-liner with real consequences for the code:
\textbf{board predicates for legality; search probes only where a
search value is genuinely needed.}  Exactly one search probe survives
in the terminal logic, and Section~\ref{sec:bandedge} explains why it
must.

\subsection{A reference specification, and a proof that the shipped
loop equals it}

The design above is easier to specify than to implement.  The
\emph{specification} wants an eager entry scan: at any king-capturable
node, return $\MU$ immediately, before the table, before the repetition
check, before anything.  The \emph{implementation} cannot afford that
--- a full legality scan at every node is not a price a 142-line Python
engine can pay --- so it earns the same guarantee lazily, through the
interception, the move order, and the killer table.

Rather than argue that the two agree, the development contains both and
proves it.  \texttt{boundD2} is the reference (and a runnable
\texttt{reference.py} accompanies it in the repository);
\texttt{boundKCX} is the shipped loop.

\begin{theorem}[Production equals reference]
\label{thm:prodeqref}
Under \texttt{KingCaptureValHigh}, \texttt{CaptureFirst} and
\texttt{KillerLegal} --- the latter two themselves theorems
(Sections~\ref{sec:killerlife},~\ref{sec:invariants}) --- the shipped
consumer and the reference compute the \emph{same function} at every
depth, position, and driver-range window.
\leanref{production\_eq\_reference}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Strong induction: children one level down, the pass three levels down.
At a king-capturable node the reference returns $\MU$ by construction;
the production loop reaches the same value by one of two routes, and
the case split is exactly the two routes --- either a virtual yield
arrives first and the interception substitutes the capture, or no
virtual yield cuts and the capture heads the sorted move list
(\texttt{CaptureFirst}) and cuts as the first searched real yield.
Everywhere else the interception and the reference's verifier make
identical decisions about the shared pass probe
(\texttt{nullArm\_match}), and the two folds agree child by child.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the machine twin of an engine-side check we ran anyway:
reference and production compared bound-for-bound over 9{,}600 probes.
Neither replaces the other --- the battery covers the Python the model
abstracts, the theorem covers the infinitely many positions the battery
does not --- but agreeing is what makes both believable.

Its immediate corollary is the invariant that started
Section~\ref{sec:refuted}: \texttt{kingCapturableReportsExact\_restored}
re-establishes, now as a theorem about the shipped consumer, the
statement Cex.~\ref{cex:kcr} refuted for the old loop.  The countermodel
stays in the development as the record of why the repair was necessary.

\subsection{Stratifying the contract, and one arithmetic identity}
\label{sec:strat}

One piece of the repair is worth isolating, because it is where a
proof directly bought back code.

The interception originally had a depth-0 arm, so that a quiescence
node could also promise the exact sentinel.  That arm is unnecessary,
and the reason is a single line of integer arithmetic.  The contract is
\emph{stratified}: at depth 0 a king-capturable node need only
\emph{fail high}, and at depth $\ge 1$ it reports the sentinel
\emph{exactly} (\texttt{kingCaptureContract\_stratified}).

The depth-0 half is free: either the stand-pat already meets the window,
or the capture does with room to spare
(\texttt{qsStrat\_failHigh\_of\_capture}).  The question is whether the
weaker leaf costs the layers above anything, and the answer is no,
because of:

\begin{theorem}[The futility test \emph{is} the child's stand-pat test]
\label{thm:futid}
For all integers,
\[
  \mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val} < \gamma
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  1 - \gamma \;\le\; -(\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}).
\]
\leanref{futility\_iff\_child\_standpat}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\texttt{omega}.
\end{proof}

The identity is trivial; its consequence is not.  Read through the
score identity of \texttt{Position.move}, it says that a parent
\emph{searches} a child exactly when that child's own stand-pat cannot
cut.  So the only depth-0 children the recursion ever reaches are those
at which the quiescence loop must run past its stand-pat --- into the
king capture, which heads the move order --- and therefore reports the
sentinel exactly anyway (\texttt{qsStrat\_exact\_of\_searched}).  The
weaker leaf is invisible to every consumer, and the interception's
depth-0 arm could be deleted.

The same identity, incidentally, is the sentence sunfish's own source
has carried for a decade as a comment justifying futility pruning:
\texttt{"pos.score + val < gamma === -(pos.score + val) >= 1-gamma"}.
It was a theorem all along.

\begin{remark}[What the split cost, and what it required]
This is also the clearest example in the campaign of the model having
to \emph{improve} rather than merely record.  With the leaf weakened,
the model's encoding of the certificate --- ``no searched real yield
beat the sentinel'' collapsed to ``the accumulator is still $\LOSS$''
--- was no longer faithful, and \texttt{stratLeaf\_needs\_futility}
machine-checks the resulting hole.  The shipped engine was never
affected (futility fires on exactly the offending child, so the yield
is virtual and the certificate stays false), but the model had to make
futility a first-class yield species before the stratified contract
could be stated honestly.  Under the repository's rule that the model
must always do what the code does, that was not optional.
\end{remark}

\section{The Last Chess Premise, and Why We Could Not Assume It}
\label{sec:bandedge}

After the repair, one chess statement remained inside the correctness
layer.  Call it \texttt{NoZugzwangInMateBand}:

\begin{quote}
\emph{If passing wins in the mate band, then some real move does too
--- you cannot be in zugzwang while delivering forced mate.}
\end{quote}

It is the mate-band fragment of the ordinary no-zugzwang assumption,
and it is needed because the interception's mate-band arm tests the
pass's \emph{report} for band membership, while reports straddle the
band across different windows: a sub-band report can be a loose bound
on a mate-band value.

It is also extremely plausible, which is why it survived several review
passes.  Delivering forced mate while having no move at all that does
so sounds self-contradictory.

It is false.  A counterexample search over generated positions,
verified against \texttt{python-chess}, produced
\texttt{8/6p1/6R1/k7/2K5/8/8/8 w}: legal, Black has exactly one reply,
passing mates immediately, and White has no forced mate within three.
Three sibling positions with the same property were found alongside it.

That is a different situation from every other named hypothesis in this
paper.  A bet you cannot discharge can be named, measured and kept; a
premise that is \emph{false} and \emph{load-bearing} cannot be kept at
all.  The choice was to remove the need for it.

\paragraph{The band-edge probe.}
When a sub-band virtual cutoff would otherwise survive, the engine
re-probes the pass at one specific window --- the band boundary
$1 - \ML$ --- and uses the direction of that report to decide.  The
window is chosen so that the report is decisive in \emph{both}
directions:

\begin{theorem}[The boundary window is decisive]
\label{thm:bandedge}
Let $r$ be any fail-soft-sound report for value $V$ at the window
$1 - \ML$, i.e.\ $1 - \ML \le r \Rightarrow r \le V$ and
$r < 1 - \ML \Rightarrow V \le r$.  Then
\[
  r < 1 - \ML \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ML \le -V .
\]
\leanref{boundary\_window\_decisive}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both directions are integer arithmetic on the two fail-soft
implications.  Left to right: a fail low gives $V \le r < 1 - \ML$,
hence $\ML \le -V$ (with $\ML \ge 0$, \texttt{omega} closes it).  Right
to left: if $r$ were $\ge 1 - \ML$ then $r \le V \le -\ML$, which
contradicts $\ML \ge 1$.  In Lean the whole proof is a
\texttt{constructor} and two \texttt{omega} calls.
\end{proof}

The point is that at a general window a report only bounds the value on
one side, so mate-band membership is undecidable from it; at
\emph{this} window the two sides meet, and the single report classifies
membership exactly.  The engine therefore \emph{certifies} what the
premise asserted, at the cost of one extra probe on a path that is
already rare.

\paragraph{The result.}
With the band-edge arm shipped, \texttt{NoZugzwangInMateBand} leaves the
premise list.  Layer 1 --- the search's bracketing property against its
own declared value function, and with it the non-crossing of the
transposition table --- \textbf{carries no chess statement at all}.
Zugzwang survives only in layer 2, as the validity region of the
null-move approximation, where it governs the \emph{accuracy} of the
value and can never make the table contradict itself.  The false
premise is retained in the development as a record, with its witness,
because ``we assumed this and it was false'' is exactly the kind of
thing a formal development should be unable to forget.

\section{The Invariant Catalogue: What Is Guaranteed, and How}
\label{sec:invariants}

This section is the paper's answer to the question a maintainer
actually asks: \emph{what does the shipped engine guarantee, what earns
each guarantee, and how do they compose?}  It is self-contained.

\subsection{The invariants}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I1.\ The search brackets its declared
    value.}}]  For every depth, position and driver-range window, the
    report $r$ satisfies $\gamma \le r \Rightarrow r \le V(p,d)$ and
    $r < \gamma \Rightarrow V(p,d) \le r$, where $V$ is the engine's
    null-inclusive declared value function --- one function determined
    by the transposition key, gamma-free and killer-free.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{bound\_null\_spec}
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:layer1}), transferred to the shipped consumer by
    \texttt{production\_eq\_reference}.  \emph{Chess premises:} none.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I2.\ The table cannot contradict
    itself.}}]  Two probes of the same $(\mathrm{pos},\mathrm{depth})$
    at any two driver-range windows never produce a fail-high report
    above a fail-low report; hence no stored entry has
    $\mathit{lower} > \mathit{upper}$.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{kcx\_no\_crossing}
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:kcxnocross}) --- an immediate corollary of I1,
    which is the entire point of insisting on a point spec.
    \emph{Chess premises:} none.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I3.\ King capture is reported
    faithfully.}}]  At depth $\ge 1$, a node whose opponent king can be
    captured returns exactly $\MU$; at depth 0 it fails high.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{kingCaptureContract\_stratified},
    assembled from \texttt{qsStrat\_failHigh\_of\_capture} (leaf) and
    \texttt{boundKCX\_capture\_exact} (interior), with
    \texttt{kingCapturableReportsExact\_restored} stating it for the
    shipped consumer.  \emph{Earned by construction} through the
    substitution arm --- this is the invariant that was refuted before
    the repair (Cex.~\ref{cex:kcr}).

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I4.\ The killer table only ever holds
    proven moves.}}]  Every \texttt{tp\_move} entry is a legal move of
    its key position, and at a king-capturable key it is itself a king
    capture --- across all store species, eviction, and successive
    searches.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{killerLegal\_lifecycle}
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:killerlife}).  \emph{Status:} was a premise, is
    now a theorem.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I5.\ Static evaluations never reach the
    mate band.}}]  No position's static score can be confused with a
    king-capture sentinel.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{evalBound\_lt\_MATE\_LOWER}, decided
    by the kernel over the transcribed piece-square tables, together
    with the $\ML = K - 13Q$ margin repair (\texttt{margin\_covers},
    Remark~\ref{rem:margin}).

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{I6.\ Every probe window is in band.}}]
    Every window the driver can ever pass to \texttt{bound} ---
    including the one carried across a depth boundary --- lies in
    $(1-\MU, \MU]$.
    \emph{Guaranteed by:} \texttt{driver\_wide\_is\_now\_the\_range}
    and \texttt{dstep\_wide\_sides}, with no clamp and no assumption
    about scores (Section~\ref{sec:mtdbi}).
\end{description}

\subsection{How they compose}

\begin{theorem}[Layer 1: the algorithm, unconditionally]
\label{thm:layer1}
For every game, guard and killer predicate satisfying
$\mathrm{KillerLegal}$, and every depth, position and window in
$(-\MU, \MU]$, the reference search brackets $\mathrm{nullValueD2}$ in
the fail-soft sense.
\leanref{bound\_null\_spec}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Strong induction on depth --- strong, because the pass option recurses
at $\mathrm{depth} - 3$ rather than $\mathrm{depth}-1$.  Depth 0 and
the two by-construction branches (king already gone; king capturable)
are reflexivity, since search and declared value agree definitionally
there.  The interesting case is a verified null cutoff, and it splits
on whether the oracle has confirmed the node terminal.  If it has, the
surviving cutoff is non-positive (\texttt{positiveNullCutoffVerified}
--- the positive case was folded away by the interception) and the
in-check sub-case is impossible outright, because an enabled pass at an
in-check node is exactly the fold identity (\texttt{nullAtMateD2}).  If
it has not, the declared function admits the pass term as its initial
accumulator and the cutoff is bounded by it --- either by the
suppression on a fail high, or by the window on a fail low.  The
remaining case is the ordinary fold, which is
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}'s argument with two additions: futile
children contribute through the virtual accumulator, and their declared
contribution is covered by \texttt{futile\_contrib\_le}, which is why
the declared function needs no gamma and stays key-determined.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No crossing]
\label{thm:kcxnocross}
Under the same premises, if a probe at $\gamma_1$ fails high and a probe
at $\gamma_2$ fails low, then
$\mathrm{bound}(p,\gamma_1,d) \le \mathrm{bound}(p,\gamma_2,d)$.
\leanref{kcx\_no\_crossing}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fail-high report is $\le V(p,d)$ and the fail-low report is
$\ge V(p,d)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:layer1}; \texttt{omega}.  The content
is entirely in there being \emph{one} $V$ --- which is what the point
spec buys and what re-search LMR could not provide
(Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Layer 2: accuracy, and the only chess assumption]
Under $\mathrm{NoZugzwang}$ --- ``the pass value never exceeds the best
real move'' --- the declared value function coincides with the
real-move value, and the search's guarantee becomes a guarantee about
chess.
\leanref{nullValue\_eq\_realValue\_of\_noZugzwang, boundKCX\_spec}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

Figure~\ref{fig:chain} shows the dependency structure.  The shape is
the point: everything a \emph{table} can depend on lives in the
chess-free layer, so a failure of the chess assumption can make the
engine play a worse move but cannot make its data structures
inconsistent.

\subsection{What this does \emph{not} say}

Three limits, stated plainly, because a catalogue like this is easy to
over-read.

First, the guarantees are about the \emph{Lean model}.  The bridge to
the Python is audit plus the automated drift guard of
Section~\ref{sec:drift}, not machine proof.

Second, the scoped abstractions of Section~\ref{sec:model-not} remain:
notably quiescence's interior is the leaf's fixpoint rather than being
unrolled, and \texttt{Position}'s internals are axiomatized by
structural properties.  ``\texttt{gen\_moves} implements chess'' is not
proved here and is the one genuinely open item
(Section~\ref{sec:lessons}).

Third, \texttt{HistoryLegal} --- that positions in the game history are
never king-capturable --- is accepted as \emph{input validity} rather
than proved: it closes the one path that precedes the consumer, since a
king-capturable position inside the repetition set would answer with a
draw score and evade I3.

The honest one-sentence summary, which is also how the pull request
describes itself: \emph{the search's bound property and its table's
consistency are machine-proven about the shipped code with no chess
assumptions, over a model that treats quiescence's interior and
\texttt{Position}'s internals as axiomatized boundaries.}  That is a
much stronger claim than the project has made before, and it is not
``the whole engine is verified''.

\section{Keeping the Model Honest}
\label{sec:drift}

A formalization of a live codebase decays unless something prevents it.
The rule this repository adopted --- \emph{the model must always do
what the code does}, with divergence treated as a blocker rather than a
footnote --- needs enforcement, and two failure modes turned out to
need different mechanisms.

\emph{Semantic drift} is caught by hashing.
\texttt{formal/scripts/model\_audit.py}, run in CI, hashes every
audited region of \texttt{sunfish.py} --- \texttt{Searcher.bound},
\texttt{Searcher.search}, the \texttt{Position} methods the model
axiomatizes, and the constants --- and fails on any change that is not
accompanied by a same-commit re-audit and hash refresh.

\emph{Citation drift} is invisible to hashing, and we only noticed
because it had already happened: a Lean comment citing
``sunfish.py:339'' is not code drift when line 339 moves, so no hash
changes, and \texttt{Killer.lean} had rotted exactly that way ---
citing 339 and 356--357 for code that had moved to 391 and 422--423.
The guard now also (a) checks that every distinctive source
\emph{anchor} the model cites by name still occurs in
\texttt{sunfish.py}, and (b) \emph{ratchets} the number of raw
line-number citations across the Lean sources so it can fall but never
rise.  New comments are expected to cite function names and distinctive
source text instead of line numbers.

Neither mechanism proves the correspondence.  They make an
\emph{undetected} divergence require someone to actively defeat CI,
which is a different risk from the one we started with.

\subsection{Shrinking a bet: the layered null-move story}
\label{sec:nullmove}

The ledger is not static; the null-move entry shows a bet being
\emph{shrunk} by restructuring (Figure~\ref{fig:nulllayers}).  Sunfish's
\texttt{can\_null} flag plays four roles at once --- null-move gate,
repetition gate, transposition-key component, and IID recursion flag ---
and \texttt{CanNull.lean} models all four exactly as master runs them
(including two audit surprises recorded there: sunfish permits
\emph{consecutive} null moves, and the move generator's laziness is
semantically load-bearing for the table state).  The old, monolithic
claim ``null-move search satisfies the docstring'' needed a zugzwang
hypothesis in front of everything.  The layered version needs it almost
nowhere:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer 1 (unconditional).}  Define
    $\mathrm{nullValue}$ --- the value function of the
    null-and-repetition-augmented search itself, with the pass option
    (searched at $\mathrm{depth}-3$ behind the gamma-free guard
    $|\mathrm{score}| < 500$) as one more option in the fold.  Then the
    search brackets $\mathrm{nullValue}$ with a \emph{point} spec, and
    the $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth}, \mathrm{can\_null})$-keyed table
    stays consistent, with no zugzwang hypothesis anywhere
    \leanref{boundNullTT\_spec, CTableOK}{CanNull.lean}.
    Self-consistency of search plus table is not a bet.
  \item \textbf{Layer 2 (the bet, now smaller).}  Relating
    $\mathrm{nullValue}$ to the null-free value is where the null-move
    bet lives: under \texttt{NullBetOK} (some real move matches the
    reduced-depth pass; the required witness also excludes guard-passing
    stalemates) and an empty history, $\mathrm{nullValue}$ collapses to
    the plain value \leanref{nullValue\_plain}{CanNull.lean}, and
    composing the layers recovers the docstring.
\end{itemize}

Zugzwang --- precisely $\neg\texttt{NullBetOK}$ --- can make the engine
compute the wrong \emph{value}, but can never make its table contradict
its search.  A satisfying corollary of the keyed-table invariant being
history-relative: the empty table satisfies it for \emph{any} history
\leanref{ctableOK\_empty}{CanNull.lean}, which is exactly why sunfish
clears \texttt{tp\_score} whenever the game history changes.

The framing, in one paragraph: the engine is \emph{allowed} to bet ---
that is what selective search is --- but every bet must have a name, a
statement in the ledger, and, where possible, a measured exposure.
Restructuring can shrink a bet (this subsection); a counterexample can
prove a bet is real (Cex.~\ref{cex:stale}); and a discharge can retire
one (Theorem~\ref{thm:futility}).  What is not allowed is an unnamed
bet.

\section{Empirical Methodology and Honest Numbers}
\label{sec:empirical}

Functional tests tell you the engine is correct; they tell you nothing
about whether a change makes it stronger.  Every strength-relevant
change in this campaign was priced by a tournament between engines
frozen with \texttt{git archive} (code \emph{and} tools --- no shared
files), playing color-swapped pairs from a random-order opening book
under fastchess~\cite{fastchess}, typically 300 games at
\texttt{tc=4+0.04} ($\approx \pm 30$ ELO at 95\%), with adjudication,
on an otherwise idle machine.  The full methodology, each rule with its
origin story, is \texttt{docs/TESTING.md} in the repository.

\paragraph{What the correctness campaign cost.}
The headline is that it cost nothing measurable.  The
verify-on-suspicion landing --- which added a legality certificate to
the fold, a three-armed validation before any virtual cutoff, a
full-coverage board-predicate scan, and later a band-edge probe ---
measures $-10.4 \pm 28.7$ over 300 games at 60+1 against the master it
replaced: indistinguishable from zero, and the point estimate is well
inside the noise floor for that sample.  The subsequent golf, deleting
the interception's depth-0 arm once Theorem~\ref{thm:futid} showed it
redundant, measures $+5.8 \pm 29.0$.  Neither number is interesting on
its own; together they say the repairs of this campaign were bought
with proofs rather than with playing strength, which is not what the
LMR story would have led us to expect.

The last row is different in kind: it is the standing price of the one
assumption the engine still makes.  Deleting the null move outright ---
which is what refusing the zugzwang bet would mean --- costs
$-34.9 \pm 40.3$.  The engine keeps the bet, the ledger keeps its name,
and the formal work's contribution is that the bet is now confined to
a layer where being wrong costs \emph{accuracy} and not
\emph{consistency}.

Three narrative points, because the numbers alone mislead:

\paragraph{The price of point-consistency.}
Re-search LMR admits no point spec (Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}), and the
interval-shaped statement we first proved in its place guarantees
nothing (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).  One can buy the point spec back
--- and each way of doing so was priced:
deterministic LMR ($-48 \pm 33$), a conservative reduction condition
($-16 \pm 38$), sticky TT entries ($-33$), ordering-only ($-34$).
Every restoration appeared ELO-negative.  Likewise, completing
null-move
soundness (searching the pass at a depth for which the spec discharges)
was priced at $-28$ and declined: the engine keeps the bet, and the
ledger keeps its name (\texttt{NullBetOK}).  The two epilogues are
instructive.  Knowing both the price \emph{and} what it buys, the
maintainer first chose to pay the smallest one --- conservative
deterministic LMR, believed ${\sim}16$ ELO for point specs end-to-end
(Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}).  Then the final campaign
(Section~\ref{sec:removal}) showed there had been nothing to pay for:
the believed $-16$ was really ${\sim}{-50}$, and the honest form of
what the point spec supposedly cost is worth exactly $0.00$.  Master
now ships no reductions at all --- the point spec holds for the code
as written, at a measured \emph{gain}.  Neither decision --- first
paying, then unwinding --- would have been coherent without both
loops: the proofs identified exactly what the ELO was buying (unsound
bound propagation), and the tournaments certified that buying it
honestly earns nothing.

\paragraph{Time-regime artifacts.}
An earlier survey measured reverse futility at $+45$ and mate-score
softening at $+19$.  After a fix to time handling (in-search deadlines
replacing between-iteration checks), both were re-measured on the fixed
baseline: $-1$ and $-22$.  The original numbers were artifacts of the
old time regime, not chess.  Rule: after any time-handling change,
re-baseline everything you believed.

\paragraph{The multi-TC lesson.}
An opening think-time ramp (cap of $0.1\mathrm{s} \times$ ply) shipped
with no cutoff and silently governed \emph{entire games} at long time
controls: at 15+3 the proper budget is ${\sim}25$s/move but the ramp
still bound at move 60, and a user's game ended in mate with 14 of 15
minutes unused.  The bug was invisible at the 4+0.04 test TC, where the
ramp stops binding at ply 2 --- it sailed through 300-game matches.  It
was caught by a user report and fixed the same day; the fix was verified
by driving the per-move time curve directly at 15+3 (17--24s observed
mid-game vs.\ 3--9s before).  This became a standing rule:
time-management changes must be validated at multiple time controls,
including a direct per-move time-curve check, because fast-TC matches
are structurally blind to long-TC time bugs.

\paragraph{Contamination stories, briefly.}
A contaminated run (engines sharing a live checkout) once measured
$-60$ ELO at LOS 0.02\% for a change later shown to search bit-identical
node counts --- hence the freeze rule and the old-vs-old control match.
Tournament managers reuse engine processes across games, so state
surviving \texttt{ucinewgame} acts as invisible cross-game learning: a
four-way eviction-policy round-robin ranked
$+66$ / $+47$ / $+15$ / $-132$ across candidates whose \emph{per-game}
search was in some cases provably identical.  Behavior-neutrality is
checked by depth-limited lockstep (moves \emph{and} node counts), never
by clock.

\subsection{The frame}

Proofs identify the trade; tournaments price it.  Neither loop alone
would have handled LMR correctly: without the proof we would not have
known the crossing existed, that no interval reading rescues it, or
that the killer and stalemate arguments survive reduction; without the
tournaments, the point spec would first have been bought blind --- and
we would never have learned that its true price was negative: the
honest variant of the reductions is worth exactly zero
(Section~\ref{sec:removal}), so the engine now ships full-width and
provable, stronger than it was with either LMR.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Nipkow's invited ITP 2024 paper~\cite{nipkow2024alphabeta} and the
accompanying Isabelle/HOL AFP entry~\cite{AlphaBetaPruningAFP} verify
fail-hard and fail-soft alpha-beta over linear orders, distributive
lattices and de Morgan domains; the fail-soft correctness statement
there is the two-sided bound that our $\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ specializes
to a null window, and our loop invariant (``the running best is a
fail-soft report of the fold so far'') follows the same shape.
\emph{Formal Verification of Minimax Algorithms}~\cite{dafnyMinimax}
verifies minimax variants with alpha-beta and transposition tables in
Dafny --- the closest prior treatment of the table invariant (our
\texttt{TableOK}).  Knuth and Moore's classical
analysis~\cite{knuth1975alphabeta} and the MTD($f$)
framework~\cite{plaat1996mtdf} supply the algorithmic background;
futility pruning in the form sunfish uses traces to
Heinz~\cite{heinz1998futility}.

Our position: to our knowledge this is the first Lean~4 formalization of
null-window/MTD-bi search, and the first line of work to pair machine
proofs of search soundness with ELO-measured prices on a production
engine --- one that plays rated games in public.  We found no prior
formal treatment of king-capture (pseudo-legal) search, of a stalemate
correction, of killer-table invariants, or of a machine-checked
refutation of transposition-table point-consistency under
gamma-adaptive Late Move Reductions (Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}).

The verified prior work above shares a convenient property that sunfish
does not have: it specifies searches over \emph{legal} move
generators, where ``no moves'' is directly observable and terminal
values need no reconstruction.  Pseudo-legal, king-capture search ---
the idiom that makes a 142-line engine possible at all --- moves
terminality from an observation to an inference, and
Section~\ref{sec:refuted} is an account of that inference going wrong
in three ways.  We are not aware of prior formal work on it, and we
suspect the ``score implies legality'' family is common in engines that
use the idiom, precisely because each instance looks locally obvious.

\section{Lessons and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:lessons}

\paragraph{The model--code gap.}
All theorems are about the Lean model of Section~\ref{sec:model}, not
about \texttt{sunfish.py}.  The gap is managed, not closed: the model
is line-mapped (Figure~\ref{fig:model}; the correspondence table in
\texttt{formal/README.md} pairs every Lean definition with the sunfish
code it transcribes), it is re-audited at every commit touching an
audited region, and CI enforces both halves of that discipline
mechanically (Section~\ref{sec:drift}).  The counterexamples are
constructed to exercise exactly the modeled mechanism, and features
excluded from a given model are excluded \emph{explicitly}, from a
closed list, with a stated reason (Section~\ref{sec:model-not}).
Still: quiescence's interior is a fixpoint at the leaf, move ordering
is modeled by its load-bearing consequences only, and \textbf{no chess
rules are formalized at all}.  A reader should trust the theorems as
statements about the search mechanisms, quantified over all abstract
games, not as end-to-end verification of the Python.

Two things would narrow the gap, and we name them because they are the
obvious next steps rather than because we have done them.  The first is
to verify \texttt{gen\_moves} against the rules of chess --- currently
assigned to a separate track that works on the Python semantics
directly, and the one item on the development's open list.  The second
is cheaper and, we suspect, higher-yield per unit effort: instantiate
the abstract \texttt{Game} with a concrete Lean chess instance and
\emph{differentially test} the Lean search against \texttt{sunfish.py}
at fixed depth.  That would not prove the correspondence, but it would
convert it from an audited claim into an empirically exercised one, on
the same footing as the 9{,}600-probe reference-versus-production
battery that already backs Theorem~\ref{thm:prodeqref}.

\paragraph{Two author claims refuted by the proof assistant,
mid-project.}
We record these because they are the strongest evidence in this paper
that the machine was doing work the authors could not do by inspection.
\emph{(1) The fail-high-vs-$V_{\mathrm{full}}$ error.}  While
specifying LMR we claimed that fail-high reports remain sound against
full $\negamax$ --- the re-search guard, we reasoned, ensures no reduced
fail high is ever yielded.  Lean refused the induction: the guard
protects only the \emph{immediately} reduced move, while a parent fail
high is certified by child fail lows, which are only sound against the
children's \emph{reduced} values --- the contamination is recursive and
cannot be scrubbed at one level.  This is why the interval-shaped
statement of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} had to make $\Vhi$'s children
$\Vlo$-valued, and why the mutually recursive pair existed at all ---
before both were deleted as guaranteeing nothing.
\emph{(2) The $V_{\mathrm{reduced}}$ target error.}  We claimed the
folklore fail-low target --- reducible moves simply valued one ply
shallower ($\mathrm{negamaxShallow}$; deleted along with the
interval-shaped statements it fed) --- was the right lower target.
Also false: on the re-search
fall-through, the yielded value bounds the \emph{deep} child value while
the shallow value has just failed high and can exceed everything
yielded; the sound per-move entry is the \emph{minimum} of the two
depths.  Both errors would have survived code review; neither survived
\texttt{lake build}.

\paragraph{Four more, from the second half.}
The pattern repeated, and the later instances are more embarrassing
than the earlier ones because by then we knew to look.
\emph{(3) The reduced correction scan.}  We proposed skipping
quiescence-filtered moves in the legality scan --- an obvious-looking
optimization on a rare path --- and wrote it.  It is unsound, and the
countermodel (\texttt{reducedScan\_needs\_premise}) is a legal child
whose own legal moves all sit below its threshold, so the filtered
value is exactly the sentinel.  The proposal was reverted; full
coverage was restored.
\emph{(4) The redundant killer lookup.}  We argued from the code that
reading \texttt{tp\_move} before the virtual yields was a wasted
dictionary access.  Measurement refuted it: because the killer table is
position-keyed and quiescence below the null move is not ply-limited,
the null subtree can transpose back to the same board and store there
--- 10 disagreements in 3.0M reads.  The lookup stayed where it was.
\emph{(5) The README's own driver claim.}  Both this paper's source and
the engine's comment asserted that MTD-bi only probes inside the mate
band.  Modeling the driver refuted it for the carried first probe of a
depth (\texttt{carried\_gamma\_escapes\_band}).  Note the direction of
the fix: the \emph{code} moved (the bracket widened) rather than the
theorems weakening.
\emph{(6) A plausible chess fact.}  ``You cannot be in zugzwang while
delivering forced mate'' survived several reviews on plausibility
alone, and is false (Section~\ref{sec:bandedge}).  We record it because
it is the case that most clearly justifies the whole exercise: no
amount of careful reading was going to catch it, and the only reason we
looked was that the formalization forced the statement to be written
down precisely enough to be searched for.

\paragraph{Measurement contamination.}
Every rule in \texttt{docs/TESTING.md} exists because its absence
produced a confident wrong number in this repository: the $-60$ ELO
phantom regression (shared checkout), the $\pm$dozens swings from
cross-game table persistence (process reuse), fictional error bars from
deterministic engines without opening books, and the long-TC time bug
invisible at fast TC.  We treat the methodology document as part of the
scientific apparatus, on the same footing as the proofs.

\paragraph{Threats.}
Single engine, one maintainer's conjectures; ELO error bars at 300
games are $\pm 30$-ish and several middle-block numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:elo} are single runs --- which in particular means the
correctness campaign's ``costs nothing'' conclusion is a statement
about a $\pm 29$ interval containing zero, not a demonstration of
exact neutrality.  The taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} is
validated on one engine family (null-window MTD-bi) and its transfer to
PVS/aspiration engines is argued, not proven.  The invariant catalogue
of Section~\ref{sec:invariants} inherits every limit of the model, and
we restate the largest one: the correspondence between the Lean
definitions and the Python is audited and CI-guarded, not proved.
Finally, the three refuted assumptions were found by targeted search
over real positions; that method establishes falsity conclusively when
it succeeds, but its failure to find a witness for a fourth assumption
would not be evidence of that assumption's truth, and we have not
treated it as such anywhere in this paper.

\section{Availability}
\label{sec:availability}

Everything is in the public sunfish
repository~\cite{sunfish}: the engine (\texttt{sunfish.py}), the Lean
development (\texttt{formal/}, building with \texttt{lake build} from a
pinned Lean 4.12.0 toolchain, zero dependencies beyond core Lean and the
\texttt{omega} tactic --- no Mathlib --- so a full build takes seconds),
the executable reference specification that Theorem~\ref{thm:prodeqref}
relates to the shipped loop, the drift guard
(\texttt{formal/scripts/model\_audit.py}, run by CI), the methodology
(\texttt{docs/TESTING.md}), and the premise inventory, correspondence
table and PR-guideline ledger (\texttt{formal/README.md}).  The
guideline is worth singling out for anyone extending the engine: it
states, per mechanism, which theorem a change must preserve --- which
is the practical form the whole campaign takes once the paper is
closed.  CI builds the proofs and plays real protocol games: fastchess
tournaments and a mock-Lichess integration test that drives the engine
over the wire exactly as the production bot is driven.  The engine
itself plays continuously on Lichess as \texttt{sunfish-engine}.
