\begin{abstract}
Sunfish is a chess engine whose whole logic fits in 131 lines of Python,
and whose docstrings state, in unusually precise English, the contracts
its search is supposed to satisfy.  We report on a one-day campaign that
treated those docstrings as latent formal specifications: we transcribed
them into Lean~4, proved the ones that are true, refuted the ones that
are not, and named the ones that are only conditionally true --- and, in
parallel, priced every resulting engine change with
ELO tournaments under a fixed empirical methodology.  The formal
deliverables include a sorry-free proof of the engine's core fail-soft
null-window contract, a machine-checked seven-position counterexample
showing the engine's stalemate correction is sound only under a named
hypothesis, a same-day proof of a maintainer-conjectured invariant about
killer moves, and a machine-checked transposition-table inconsistency
induced by gamma-adaptive Late Move Reductions.  For the latter we
first proved an interval-shaped statement --- fail highs bound one
value function, fail lows another, the truth in between --- and then
deleted it: such a claim is a guarantee only if the gap between the two
functions is bounded, and no bound on search instability is provable
(one ply of depth can hide a mate), so the interval reading was never a
specification, only a description of a bug.  The only contract the
engine recognizes is the point spec: every stored bound describes one
value function determined by the transposition key.  These results organize into a soundness
taxonomy for selective search: the search window may shape termination,
and may trigger shortcuts whose value provably one-side-bounds the same
value function; it must never select between incomparable evaluations.
Each unproven assumption is kept as a named hypothesis --- a ledger of
bets --- and, where possible, measured on the production engine, which
plays publicly on Lichess.  Proofs identify the trade; tournaments price
it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sunfish\footnote{\url{https://github.com/thomasahle/sunfish}} is a
minimalist chess engine: with comments and whitespace removed, the
engine proper is 131 lines of Python, yet it plays at ratings above 2000
on Lichess, where it runs in production as the bot
\texttt{sunfish-engine}.  What makes it an unusual verification target
is not its size but its \emph{documentation}: the docstring of its
central routine, \texttt{Searcher.bound}, is a complete two-sided
fail-soft contract, stated a decade before anyone tried to prove it.
The comments scattered through the search --- ``\texttt{lower <= s(pos)
<= upper}'', ``the opponent will for sure just stand pat'', ``sunfish
may report `mate', but then after more search realize it's not a mate
after all'' --- turn out to be, respectively: a table invariant, a
dischargeable lemma, and the exact failure mode of a hypothesis that a
machine-checked counterexample shows to be necessary.  The docstrings
were latent formal specs.

This paper reports a campaign, conducted in a single day on the live
repository, that ran two loops in parallel:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The proof loop.}  Every search feature was transcribed
    into a small, dependency-free Lean~4 model (core Lean plus the
    \texttt{omega} tactic; no Mathlib; a full build takes seconds) and
    either proven sorry-free, refuted by an explicit machine-checked
    counterexample, or reduced to a \emph{named hypothesis} whose
    statement --- not whose proof --- is the deliverable.
  \item \textbf{The price loop.}  Every engine change motivated by the
    proofs (and every change the proofs advised against) was priced by
    an ELO tournament between frozen engine builds, under the
    methodology of Section~\ref{sec:empirical}, on the same engine that
    plays real games on Lichess.
\end{itemize}

The two loops answer different questions and neither substitutes for the
other.  A proof tells you \emph{what} a pruning trick costs in
soundness --- which invariant it weakens, at which positions, under
which window.  A tournament tells you what that weakening costs (or
earns) \emph{in ELO}.  Several of our measured numbers
(Table~\ref{tab:elo}) show soundness restorations that are strictly ELO-negative,
and unsound-in-the-point-sense optimizations that are strongly
ELO-positive; the formal work is what lets us merge the latter with our
eyes open, with a two-line containment for the specific inconsistency
the proof predicts --- and, once the interval reading of that
containment was recognized as guaranteeing nothing, to retire the
optimization deliberately at a measured price
(Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A sorry-free Lean~4 proof that sunfish's fail-soft null-window
search satisfies its own docstring (Section~\ref{sec:contract}) --- to
our knowledge the first Lean~4 formalization of null-window/MTD-bi
search.
(2) A faithful model of the engine's king-capture convention and
stalemate correction, with a proof under four individually necessary
hypotheses and a machine-checked counterexample showing the key
hypothesis cannot be dropped (Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}).
(3) A same-day proof of a maintainer-conjectured invariant: the stored
killer move at a king-capturable position is itself a king capture
(Section~\ref{sec:killer}).
(4) A soundness taxonomy for selective search
(Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), with futility pruning and Late Move
Reductions as the canonical citizens of its two nontrivial classes.
(5) The ledger-of-bets discipline (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}) and the
empirical methodology with honest numbers, including artifacts we
initially believed (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}).

\paragraph{Reading guide.}
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines, once and explicitly, the model in which
every theorem of this paper is stated --- what is a Lean definition,
what is a named hypothesis, what is not modeled at all --- so that each
later ``Theorem'' can be read with its exact scope in mind.
Section~\ref{sec:inventory} then inventories every machine-checked
result: Table~\ref{tab:theorems} lists the proved theorems with their
Lean names and files, and Table~\ref{tab:cex} lists the machine-checked
\emph{counterexamples}, which are deliverables of equal rank.  The
remaining sections tell the story of the main results in the order they
were found.

\section{The Model: What the Theorems Quantify Over}
\label{sec:model}

Every result in this paper is a theorem about a small Lean~4 development
(\texttt{formal/} in the sunfish repository: nine files, no dependencies
beyond core Lean and the \texttt{omega} tactic), not about the Python
text of \texttt{sunfish.py}.  This section fixes the model once, so that
each later theorem can be read with its exact scope in mind; how the
model--code gap is managed is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:lessons},
and Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows the correspondence at a glance.

\subsection{Abstract games, king-capture legality, and the true value}
\label{sec:model-games}

The base structure is chess-free:

\begin{definition}[Game]
\label{def:game}
A \emph{game} $G$ consists of a type of positions $\mathrm{Pos}$, a
successor function $\mathrm{moves} : \mathrm{Pos} \to
\mathrm{List\ Pos}$, and a static evaluation $\mathrm{eval} :
\mathrm{Pos} \to \mathbb{Z}$.  The empty move list means \emph{the side
to move has lost}.  \leanref{Game}{GameTree.lean}
\end{definition}

Sunfish is a \emph{king-capture} engine: it generates pseudo-legal
moves, and legality is enforced one ply late by making king capture
decisive.  The model adopts this convention wholesale: a position with
$\mathrm{eval}(p) \le -\ML$ has already lost its king, and every search
in the development normalizes such positions to the exact sentinel
$-\MU$.  ``Check'', ``mate'' and ``stalemate'' are therefore not
primitives of the model; they are emergent, exactly as in the engine
(Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}).  Features that need more structure extend
$\mathrm{Game}$ conservatively: a \emph{NullGame} adds a pass move
$\mathrm{pass} : \mathrm{Pos} \to \mathrm{Pos}$ (the model of
\texttt{pos.rotate(nullmove=True)}); a \emph{ValGame} adds per-move
values $\mathrm{val}$ together with sunfish's incremental-score identity
$\mathrm{eval}(m) = -(\mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p,m))$ as a
structural field (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}); killer- and
transposition-table models additionally assume decidable equality of
positions, for table keying.

The ``true score'' $s^*$ of the docstring is \emph{defined} to be
depth-limited negamax: $\negamax_d(p) = \mathrm{eval}(p)$ at $d = 0$,
otherwise the maximum of $-\negamax_{d-1}(m)$ over
$m \in \mathrm{moves}(p)$, folded from $\LOSS = -\MU$
\leanref{negamax}{GameTree.lean}.  Variants of the value function ---
$\negamaxD$ (draw-aware, Section~\ref{sec:stalemate}),
$\mathrm{negamaxDet}$ (per-move reduced depths,
Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}), $\mathrm{nullValue}$ (null-move- and
repetition-augmented, Section~\ref{sec:nullmove}) --- are likewise
definitions, not axioms.  (A fourth variant, the $(\Vhi, \Vlo)$ pair
once defined for re-search LMR, has been \emph{deleted} from the
development; Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} tells that story and states
the doctrine behind the deletion.)

\subsection{The searches are Lean functions, one per feature}
\label{sec:model-searches}

\texttt{Searcher.bound} is modeled as a total recursive Lean function
mirroring the Python control flow, and each search feature is modeled in
its own file as a variant of that function: the bare fail-soft skeleton
$\mathrm{bound}$ \leanref{bound}{Bound.lean}; table threading
($\mathrm{boundTT}$), futility ($\mathrm{boundFut}$) and mate-score
softening in \texttt{Tricks.lean}; the stalemate correction
($\mathrm{boundStale}$, \texttt{Stalemate.lean}); the killer table as
state ($\mathrm{boundKill}$, \texttt{Killer.lean}); deterministic LMR as
shipped on master ($\mathrm{boundLmrDet}$, \texttt{LmrDet.lean}) and the
historical re-search LMR ($\mathrm{boundLmr}$, \texttt{Lmr.lean}); and the
\texttt{can\_null}-aware search with null move, repetition check, keyed
table and IID ($\mathrm{boundNullTT}$, \texttt{CanNull.lean}).  Studying
features in separate models is itself a modeling decision, and where
features could interact the development proves they do not: futility
(depth $\le 1$) and LMR (depth $\ge 3$) never co-occur
\leanref{futilityLmrDisjoint}{Lmr.lean}, and reductions never touch king
captures \leanref{RedRespectsCaptures}{Lmr.lean}, which is what lets the
killer and stalemate arguments survive under LMR.

\subsection{Nothing is axiomatized; conditions are named hypotheses}
\label{sec:model-axioms}

The development contains no \texttt{axiom} declarations and no
\texttt{sorry}.  Everything above is a Lean \emph{definition}; every
theorem is proven from those definitions, and \texttt{\#print axioms}
on the main theorems reports only \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound} (the ambient axioms of Lean's kernel).  Where a
claim about the engine is only \emph{conditionally} true, the condition
is not assumed globally: it is a \emph{named hypothesis carried in the
theorem statement} --- e.g.\ \emph{Bounded} (evaluations lie in
$[-\MU,\MU]$), \emph{CheckProbeOK}, \emph{MateValuesAreKingCaptures},
\emph{NullBetOK}, \emph{FutilityMateOK} --- and
Section~\ref{sec:ledger} maintains the full ledger.  Machine-checked
counterexamples are concrete finite games (three to seven positions)
whose search values are computed by Lean's kernel via \texttt{decide};
they are theorems of the form ``this universally quantified statement is
false'', not informal counterarguments.

\subsection{What is \emph{not} modeled}
\label{sec:model-not}

Theorems quantify over \emph{all} games of
Definition~\ref{def:game}; sunfish's chess is one intended instance, but
board representation and move generation are entirely abstract.
Explicitly outside the model, each with the reason the omission is
harmless (audited against master at commit \texttt{9b1a7b4}, sunfish.py
lines 286--448):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Move generation and the board.}  $\mathrm{moves}$ is an
    opaque function; no chess rules are formalized.  Consequently the
    theorems cannot certify that \texttt{gen\_moves} implements chess
    --- only how the search treats whatever moves it is given.
  \item \textbf{The quiescence interior.}  Depth 0 returns
    $\mathrm{eval}$ directly; QS's stand-pat-plus-captures recursion is
    collapsed into the evaluation.  Its one load-bearing property, the
    stand-pat/score identity, is retained as the \emph{ValGame}
    structure and is exactly what the futility proof consumes
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:futility}).
  \item \textbf{Timing and aborts.}  The deadline exception
    \texttt{Stop} raises at node \emph{entry}, before any store, so an
    abort can leave a search unfinished but never a table entry
    unjustified --- table invariants cannot be corrupted, and time is
    otherwise irrelevant to the specs.
  \item \textbf{Table eviction.}  \texttt{TABLE\_SIZE} eviction only
    forgets entries, which trivially preserves every table invariant
    stated here.
  \item \textbf{Move ordering}, except its load-bearing consequence:
    king captures (value $\ge \ML$) sort strictly first
    \leanref{orderedMoves}{Killer.lean}.
  \item \textbf{The iterative-deepening driver's scheduling} (when to
    stop deepening, time allocation).  The driver's \emph{bracket
    logic} is modeled --- it is exactly what
    Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec} serves (Section~\ref{sec:mtdbi}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The specification, transcribed verbatim}

\begin{quote}\ttfamily
Let s* be the "true" score of the sub-tree we are searching.\\
The method returns r, where\\
if gamma >~ s* then s* <= r < gamma\hspace{1em}(A better upper bound)\\

\begin{definition}[BoundSpec, the docstring]
\label{def:boundspec}
For a game $G$, depth $d$, position $p$, window $\gamma$ and report $r$,
\[
  \mathrm{BoundSpec}(G,d,p,\gamma,r) \;:\equiv\;
  \bigl(\gamma \le r \rightarrow r \le \negamax_d(p)\bigr)
  \;\wedge\;
  \bigl(r < \gamma \rightarrow \negamax_d(p) \le r\bigr).
\]
\leanref{BoundSpec}{Bound.lean}
\end{definition}

The search itself, stripped to its logical skeleton, is the fail-soft
loop with the early cutoff on $\mathit{best} \ge \gamma$, over children
searched with the flipped null window $1-\gamma$ one ply shallower
\leanref{bound, searchMoves}{Bound.lean}.  No table, no null move, no
quiescence, no killer: those return in
Sections~\ref{sec:stalemate}--\ref{sec:ledger}.

\begin{theorem}[The docstring is true]
\label{thm:bound-spec}
For every game $G$, depth $d$, position $p$ and window
$\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}$,
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}\bigl(G,d,p,\gamma,\mathrm{bound}_d(p,\gamma)\bigr)$
holds.
\leanref{bound\_spec}{Bound.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on depth.  The loop is handled by a single invariant lemma
\leanref{searchMoves\_spec}{Bound.lean}: \emph{the running
$\mathit{best}$ is itself a fail-soft-correct report of the running true
maximum}.  On a cutoff, $\mathit{best} \ge \gamma$ came either from the
current child (whose report is $\le$ its true value by the induction
hypothesis) or from the previous $\mathit{best}$ (bounded by the
invariant); either way the fold over the unsearched tail can only grow.
Without a cutoff, both the child and the previous $\mathit{best}$ failed
low, so both upper-bound their true counterparts and the invariant is
re-established.  The null-window step is the integer fact
$b < 1-\gamma \iff \gamma \le -b$: a child failing low against
$1-\gamma$ fails high for the parent, and vice versa.  The proof is
sorry-free; \texttt{\#print axioms} reports only \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this is the load-bearing spec: the MTD-bi driver}
\label{sec:mtdbi}

Sunfish's driver \texttt{Searcher.search} is MTD-bi (a bisecting variant
of MTD($f$)~\cite{plaat1996mtdf}): at each depth it maintains a bracket
$\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{score} \le \mathit{upper}$, initialized to
$[-\ML, \ML]$, and repeatedly probes \texttt{bound} at
$\gamma = \lfloor(\mathit{lower}+\mathit{upper}+1)/2\rfloor$, stopping
when the bracket is narrower than \texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS} ($=15$
centipawns) --- Figure~\ref{fig:mtdbi}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec} is exactly the property the binary search
needs: \emph{every} probe, whatever its $\gamma$, moves one end of the
bracket --- a fail high raises $\mathit{lower}$ to $r$, a fail low
lowers $\mathit{upper}$ to $r$, and the two can never cross
(Theorem~\ref{thm:no-crossing} below makes the no-crossing claim
formal, and Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} shows how re-search Late Move
Reductions break precisely it).  Fail-softness is what makes bisection
converge fast in practice: the report $r$ typically overshoots
$\gamma$, so the bracket shrinks by more than half.  Two details of the
driver are load-bearing for later sections: the probed window range
stays inside $(-\MU, \MU]$ --- an interval closed under the null-window
flip $\gamma \mapsto 1 - \gamma$ --- which
Theorems~\ref{thm:boundstale} and~\ref{thm:killer} require; and the
bracket invariant is exactly what the LMR crossing counterexample
(Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}) breaks --- and, because the spread of the
crossing reports admits no provable bound, there is no slack (not
\texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS}, not re-probing) that can honestly be said to
absorb it (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).

\section{The King-Capture Model and the Stalemate Correction}
\label{sec:stalemate}

Sunfish generates \emph{pseudo-legal} moves and scores an actual king
capture as an immediate win: a position whose static score is
$\le -\ML$ has already lost its king and is normalized to exactly
$-\MU$.  ``No legal moves'' (mate \emph{or} stalemate) therefore
surfaces in the search as $\mathit{best} = -\MU$ after the loop, and the
engine post-processes it (at $\mathrm{depth} > 2$) with an in-check
probe on the null-moved position:
$\mathit{best} := -\ML$ if in check, else $0$.

\begin{definition}[Draw-aware value]
$\negamaxD_d(p)$ is negamax over the king-capture-normalized game with
the depth-gated stalemate correction applied to the fold: if
$d > 2$ and the fold equals $\LOSS$, the value becomes $-\ML$ if $p$ is
in check and $0$ otherwise. \leanref{negamaxDraw, drawFix}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CheckProbeOK]
A probe function agrees pointwise with the one-ply king-capture reading
of check: after passing the turn, the opponent has a move reaching a
position with $\mathrm{eval} \le -\ML$.  For the real engine this is the
assumption that the depth-0 QS probe
\texttt{bound(flipped, MATE\_UPPER, 0) == MATE\_UPPER} decides check ---
true because quiescence never prunes king captures.
\leanref{CheckProbeOK, inCheckB}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[MateValuesAreKingCaptures]
\label{hyp:mvakc}
Whenever $\negamaxD_d(p) = \MU$ with $d \ge 1$, some move of $p$
actually reaches a position with $\mathrm{eval} \le -\ML$: mate-band
sentinel values always come from a real king capture, never from a
shallow-horizon artifact.
\leanref{MateValuesAreKingCaptures}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[The corrected search satisfies the docstring]
\label{thm:boundstale}
Let $G$ be a game with a pass move, and suppose:
(i) all evaluations lie in $[-\MU, \MU]$ (\emph{Bounded});
(ii) the probe satisfies \emph{CheckProbeOK};
(iii) the window is in band: $-\MU < \gamma \le \MU$ (an interval closed
under the null-window flip $\gamma \mapsto 1-\gamma$); and
(iv) \emph{MateValuesAreKingCaptures}.
Then the stalemate-corrected search $\mathrm{boundStale}$ satisfies
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ against $\negamaxD$ at every depth and position.
\leanref{boundStale\_spec}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The same loop invariant as Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}, plus a
pointwise lemma \leanref{staleFix\_spec\_core}{Stalemate.lean} showing
the correction maps a fail-soft-correct pair (search $\mathit{best}$,
true fold) to a fail-soft-correct pair.  The crux is the masking step:
if the \emph{true} fold is $\LOSS$, then (by the band, the sentinel
invariant, and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:mvakc}) every child is an exact
king-capture mate, every child report is exactly $\LOSS$, and hence the
search's $\mathit{best}$ is $\LOSS$ too --- so both sides apply the
\emph{same} correction, using the correct probe.  In-band windows are
needed so that a fail-low at $\LOSS$ certifies the loop ran to
completion: a $\gamma \le -\MU$ would allow an early cutoff at
$\LOSS$ with unsearched moves remaining.  Sorry-free.
\end{proof}

Each hypothesis is individually necessary; the interesting one is (iv):

\begin{counterexample}[The correction is not unconditionally sound]
\label{cex:stale}
There is a seven-position game with bounded honest evaluations, a
perfectly correct probe, and an in-band window $\gamma = -5$, on which
the faithful depth-gated correction returns a fail-low ``upper bound''
of $-20$ while the draw-aware value is $0$.  The mechanism: two
positions are terminal stalemates; seen at remaining depth $\le 2$ (gate
off) they score $\LOSS$, so their parents at depth 2 score a fabricated
$\MU$ --- mate scores with no king capture anywhere behind them --- and
the root inherits a wrong bound.  This is sunfish's own code comment
(``sunfish may report `mate', but then after more search realize it's
not a mate after all'') turned into a machine-checked refutation.
\leanref{boundStale\_not\_unconditional, Cex, cex\_violates\_hypothesis}{Stalemate.lean}
\end{counterexample}

position $g$ with $\negamaxD_2(g) = \LOSS$ and $\negamaxD_3(g) = 0$.

depth.

\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Why mate is $-\ML$, not $-\MU$]
$-\MU$ is the reserved sentinel meaning ``the king is \emph{actually}
capturable/gone''.  Scoring checkmate-by-correction as $-\MU$ would
re-inject the sentinel at positions whose king is not capturable and
destroy the parent-level detection one ply up; it would also order
``mated next move'' above ``king already lost''.  The engine's choice of
$-\ML$ at line 412 is what keeps the sentinel trustworthy.

\end{remark}

\section{The Killer Invariant: Conjectured at Noon, Proved by Evening}
\label{sec:killer}

While modeling the stalemate correction we recorded a potential gap: the
killer move (the stored \texttt{tp\_move} entry) is yielded \emph{before}
the sorted moves, so a non-capture killer failing high could let
\texttt{bound} return a value below $\MU$ at a king-capturable position
--- breaking the sentinel requirement that
Theorem~\ref{thm:boundstale} leans on.  The maintainer conjectured this
cannot happen in sunfish.  The conjecture was proved the same day.

\begin{definition}[KillerOK, the table invariant]
A killer table $t$ (a partial map from positions to moves) satisfies
$\mathrm{KillerOK}$ if every stored entry is a legal move of its key
position, and at a king-capturable position the stored entry --- if any
--- is \emph{itself a king capture}.
\leanref{KillerOK}{Killer.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[KillerIsKingCapture]
\label{thm:killer}
Thread \texttt{tp\_move} through the search as state (position-keyed,
store-on-fail-high-only), with the move loop running over
value-ordered moves (king captures, value $\ge \ML$, strictly first).
Then for every depth $d$, position $p$, in-band window
$-\MU < \gamma \le \MU$, and table $t$ satisfying $\mathrm{KillerOK}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the call $\mathrm{boundKill}_d(p, \gamma, t)$ returns a table
    satisfying $\mathrm{KillerOK}$; and
  \item if $d \ge 1$, $p$'s own king is not already gone, and $p$ is
    king-capturable, the returned value is \emph{exactly} $\MU$ --- on
    the killer path and on the loop path alike.
\end{enumerate}
\leanref{boundKill\_spec}{Killer.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on depth, cases on the table lookup.  \emph{Killer-cutoff
case}: the stored move is the killer itself; by the invariant it is a
king capture whenever $p$ is capturable, the king-capture normalization
scores it at the full sentinel, and an in-band $\gamma$ makes the
sentinel fail high --- so the cutoff both stores a king capture and
reports exactly $\MU$.  \emph{Killer-fail-low case}: a king-capture
killer would have reported $\MU \ge \gamma$, so $p$ is not capturable
and plain loop preservation applies.  \emph{No-killer case}: at a
capturable position the capture heads the ordered list, cuts off
immediately at the sentinel, and nothing else can be stored first
\leanref{killLoop\_capture\_head}{Killer.lean}.  Applied along any
execution history starting from the empty table
\leanref{killerEmpty\_OK}{Killer.lean}, the invariant holds forever.
Sorry-free; axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} only.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact sentinel; fail-low certifies non-capturability]
At any king-capturable node (depth $\ge 1$, in-band window) the search
reports exactly $\MU$; contrapositively, any in-band report
${} < \MU$ --- in particular any fail low --- certifies that no king
capture was available.  This is what makes the depth-0 stalemate probe
of Section~\ref{sec:stalemate} meaningful.
\end{corollary}

Three conditions are load-bearing, and each is an edit a well-meaning PR
could make:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Position-exact keying.}  Every store into
    $\texttt{tp\_move}[p]$ happens inside a call at $p$ itself; a
    ply-shared killer table (a common engine idiom) would let a quiet
    killer harvested at one position cut off at a different,
    king-capturable one, breaking the induction.
  \item \textbf{Captures sort first.}  King captures have
    $\mathrm{value} \ge \ML$, strictly above every quiet move.
  \item \textbf{In-band windows.}  An out-of-band $\gamma > \MU$ could
    let a quiet move cut off (and be stored) above the capture's
    sentinel.
\end{itemize}

The residual exception is the null-move yield: it stores nothing (so
$\mathrm{KillerOK}$ survives) but can end the loop below the sentinel;
sunfish guards it only by $|\mathrm{pos.score}| < 500$, a guard its own
FIXME concedes is heuristic.  The condition under which the guard closes
the hole is the named hypothesis
\leanref{NullGuardBlocksAtCaptures}{Killer.lean}.

\paragraph{Empirical companion.}  Before the proof existed, the
conjecture was checked empirically on the production engine:
\textbf{0 violations in 694{,}533 killer cutoffs over 1{,}270 real-game
positions}.  The proof upgraded ``empirically absent'' to ``impossible
given in-band windows and value ordering'' --- and, usefully, told us
exactly which future edits would void the guarantee.

\section{A Soundness Taxonomy for Selective Search}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

The campaign's conceptual yield is a classification of how a selective
search may consult its window $\gamma$.  Sunfish's maintainer stated it
as a principle, which we quote verbatim:

\begin{quote}
``$\gamma$ may shape termination, and may trigger shortcuts whose value
provably one-side-bounds the same function; it must never select between
incomparable evaluations.''
\end{quote}

The three classes, each with its canonical citizen:

\subsection{Class (a): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-shaped
termination --- always sound}

The beta cutoff itself: $\gamma$ decides only \emph{when the loop
stops}, and fail-soft reporting keeps every report a bound on the same
$\negamax$.  This is Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}; nothing further is
needed.  Null-window search, iterative deepening, and aspiration
re-probing all live here.

\subsection{Class (b): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-triggered
shortcuts that one-side-bound the same function --- sound}

Futility pruning is the canonical citizen.  At $\mathrm{depth} \le 1$,
once $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m) < \gamma$, sunfish answers
with the \emph{static estimate} $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m)$
instead of searching the child.  The estimate is below $\gamma$, so it
is only ever a fail-low report, and $\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ demands of a
fail-low report exactly one inequality:
$-\negamax_d(m) \le \mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val}(m)$.

We initially stated that inequality as a hypothesis
(\emph{FutilityOK}).  It was then \emph{discharged}: sunfish builds
child positions incrementally, and the score identity
\[
  \mathrm{eval}(m) \;=\; -\bigl(\mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p, m)\bigr)
  \qquad (m \in \mathrm{moves}(p))
\]
is literal in \texttt{Position.move}.  With the identity as a
structural property of the model \leanref{ValGame}{Tricks.lean}, the
futility test $\mathrm{pos.score} + \mathrm{val} < \gamma$ is
integer-equivalent to ``the child's stand-pat meets its window
$1-\gamma$'', at depth 0 the stand-pat is yielded first and fails high
immediately, and fail-soft returns exactly the child's score:

\begin{theorem}[FutilityOK discharged --- as an equality]
\label{thm:futility}
In any game with the score identity, for every position $p$ and move
$m \in \mathrm{moves}(p)$,
\[
  -\negamax_0(m) \;=\; \mathrm{eval}(p) + \mathrm{val}(p, m).
\]
The futility yield is not an estimate: it \emph{equals} the depth-0
child search it replaces.
\leanref{futilityOK\_discharged}{Tricks.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Futility-pruned search satisfies BoundSpec]
\label{thm:boundfut}
For any game with the score identity, under in-band windows and the
remaining hypothesis \emph{FutilityMateOK} (a move valued as a king
capture, $\mathrm{val} \ge \ML$, really wins: the fail-high
\texttt{else MATE\_UPPER} bypass), the futility-pruned search satisfies
$\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ against plain $\negamax$ --- a point spec on a
single value function.
\leanref{boundFut\_spec}{Tricks.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At depth 1 (the futility zone) each per-move yield is analyzed by cases:
a futile quiet move yields exactly $-\mathrm{eval}(m)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:futility}; a futile king capture yields the exact
sentinel, a fail high justified by \emph{FutilityMateOK} and the in-band
window; a non-futile move is an exact depth-0 child.  A
member-restricted variant of the loop lemma
\leanref{searchMoves\_spec\_mem}{Tricks.lean} (the score identity only
speaks about legal moves) closes the loop; deeper depths are
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound-spec}'s argument verbatim.  Sorry-free.
\end{proof}

Fine print made explicit by the model: the \emph{quantified} FutilityOK
(over all depths) is not dischargeable --- plain $\negamax$ at
$d \ge 1$ has no stand-pat option --- but the search only ever consumes
the $d = 0$ instance, and that instance is the score identity on the
nose.  The original statement over-required.

\subsection{Class (c): \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}-selected
incomparable evaluations --- unsound in the point sense}

\emph{Re-search} Late Move Reductions --- the gamma-adaptive variant
that shipped between commits \texttt{58883ea} and \texttt{7f9f164}, and
whose formal record is kept in \texttt{Lmr.lean} --- is the canonical
citizen.  A late quiet move at $\mathrm{depth} \ge 3$ is probed at
$\mathrm{depth}-2$; a reduced fail low is yielded as-is, a reduced fail
high is re-searched at full depth and only that result is yielded.  Here $\gamma$ \emph{selects which of
two incomparable value functions the yield is a fact about}, and the
docstring becomes unprovable as stated: no single value function backs
both sides.

Our first reaction was to retreat to an interval-shaped statement.
Define the mutually recursive pair $(\Vhi, \Vlo)$ with side
alternation:
\begin{align*}
  \Vhi_d(p) &= \max_{m}\; -\Vlo_{d-1}(m)
    && \text{(all full depth)}\\
  \Vlo_d(p) &= \max_{m}\;
    \begin{cases}
      \min\bigl(-\Vhi_{d-2}(m),\, -\Vhi_{d-1}(m)\bigr)
        & \text{if $m$ reducible}\\
      -\Vhi_{d-1}(m) & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
over an abstract reducibility predicate
$\mathrm{red}(d, i, m)$ generalizing sunfish's
\texttt{depth >= 3 and i\_m >= 5 and val < QS}.  We proved,
machine-checked and sorry-free at the time (as \texttt{boundLmr\_spec}
and \texttt{lmrVal\_sandwich}), that every fail-high report of the LMR
search is $\le \Vhi_d(p)$, every fail-low report is $\ge \Vlo_d(p)$,
and $\Vlo_d(p) \le \negamax_d(p) \le \Vhi_d(p)$ pointwise --- and, for
a while, we presented this pair of statements as ``the honest
replacement'' for the docstring: an \emph{interval spec}, with the
truth trapped in between.

The verdict must be stated as plainly as the retreat was: \textbf{it
was never a spec}, and the statements --- together with the
$(\Vhi,\Vlo)$ definition itself --- have been \emph{deleted} from the
development.  A claim of the form ``fail highs bound $\Vhi$, fail lows
bound $\Vlo$, and the truth lies in between'' is a guarantee only if
the gap $\Vhi - \Vlo$ is bounded.  No bound on search instability is
provable: one ply of depth can hide a mate, so the gap is unbounded
precisely in the positions that decide games.  A guarantee relative to
an uncontrollable gap guarantees nothing; the interval reading was only
a description of the bug --- in the maintainer's phrase, a ``cope
spec''.  The only contract the repository recognizes is the
\emph{point} spec: every stored bound describes one value function
determined by the transposition key.  What \texttt{Lmr.lean} keeps is
exactly what supports that doctrine: the re-search definition itself
\leanref{boundLmr}{Lmr.lean} and the machine-checked counterexample
below.

Two facts about modeling the since-deleted statements were
\emph{surprises} --- both were claims the authors believed and the
proof assistant refused; we recount them honestly in
Section~\ref{sec:lessons}.

The practical consequence is a transposition-table pathology:

\begin{theorem}[No crossing without LMR]
\label{thm:no-crossing}
For the unreduced search, a fail-high report can never exceed a fail-low
report at the same $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth})$: both bracket
$\negamax_d(p)$, so a \texttt{tp\_score} entry always satisfies
$\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{upper}$.
\leanref{bound\_no\_crossing}{Lmr.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{counterexample}[TT crossing under LMR, machine-checked]
\label{cex:crossing}
There is a three-position game and windows $\gamma = -60, 0$ on which
the LMR search at the same $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth})$ produces a
fail-high report of $+10$ and a fail-low report of $-50$: a
\texttt{tp\_score} entry with $\mathit{lower} = 10 > -50 =
\mathit{upper}$.  The single root move looks bad shallow but is good
deep; probing $\gamma = 0$ takes the reduced branch and fails low at
$-50$, probing $\gamma = -60$ bypasses it, re-searches at full depth and
fails high at $+10$.
\leanref{lmr\_tt\_crossing}{Lmr.lean}
\end{counterexample}

MTD-bi's bracket $\mathit{lower} \le \mathit{score} \le \mathit{upper}$
is therefore simply broken under re-search LMR: the gap between the two
value functions admits no provable bound, so there is no theorem of the
form ``the crossing stays within what \texttt{EVAL\_ROUGHNESS} and
re-probing absorb'' --- in the positions that decide games, it need
not.  On the production engine the crossing is real but rare:
\textbf{0.016\% of nodes} measured over real games.  The two-line
store clamp shipped at the time --- on store, widen the stale side of
the entry instead of asserting a contradiction --- measured
ELO-neutral at $+12 \pm 28$; but it was containment, not repair: it
kept crossing reports from being \emph{stored} (proved at the time as
\texttt{clamp\_no\_crossing}) while the search went on consuming them.
The \texttt{IntervalTableOK} invariant that once dressed the clamp up
as preserving a guarantee was deleted along with the interval reading
--- an invariant stated relative to an unboundable gap certifies
nothing --- and the clamp model (\texttt{TableClamp.lean}) has since
been deleted from the development entirely; git is the archive.
Killer and
stalemate lemmas are unaffected: king captures have
$\mathrm{val} \ge \ML > \mathit{QS} = 40$ and are never reducible
\leanref{RedRespectsCaptures}{Lmr.lean}; the killer is pre-loop and
structurally never reduced; reduced fail lows are $< \gamma$ and never
reach the \texttt{tp\_move} store; and the $-\MU$ king-loss sentinel is
depth-independent, so stalemate detection still sees exact sentinels
from reduced searches.

\subsection{Epilogue on master: deterministic LMR restores the point
spec}
\label{sec:lmrdet}

The story has a resolution.  As of commit \texttt{7f9f164}, master
retires the re-search variant and ships \emph{deterministic} LMR: the
reduction depends only on $(\mathrm{depth}, \mathrm{index},
\mathrm{val})$ --- \texttt{LMR = int(depth >= 4 and i\_m >= 8 and val <
0)} --- and each move is searched \emph{once}, at
$\mathrm{depth} - 1 - \mathrm{LMR}$.  Because $\gamma$ no longer selects
between evaluations, the search is back in class (b$'$): it computes
exact fail-soft bounds on a \emph{single} value function
$\mathrm{negamaxDet}$ (reducible moves valued one ply shallower,
recursively), with a point spec
\leanref{boundLmrDet\_spec}{LmrDet.lean}; fail-high reports can never
exceed fail-low reports at the same key, so transposition entries are
contradiction-free by construction
\leanref{boundLmrDet\_no\_crossing}{LmrDet.lean}; and the store clamp of
Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}'s containment became a provable no-op
(\texttt{clamp\_noop\_high/low}, proven at the time) and was removed
from the engine --- after which the no-op theorems and the whole clamp
model (\texttt{TableClamp.lean}) were themselves deleted from the
development, under the maintainer's principle that git is the archive
and the code carries no historical artifacts.  The
price was measured, not guessed: the gamma-adaptive re-search variant is
about 16 ELO stronger ($-16 \pm 38$ direct; both are large wins over no
LMR --- Table~\ref{tab:elo}).  That trade --- 16 ELO for end-to-end
point specs on single value functions --- was made deliberately;
\texttt{Lmr.lean} keeps the re-search definition and the crossing
counterexample as the standing demonstration of what the 16 ELO would
cost.  Note that $\mathrm{negamaxDet} \ne \negamax$: deterministic
LMR is honest about \emph{which} value function it computes bounds on,
rather than unsound about the original one.

\section{The Ledger of Bets: Named Hypotheses}
\label{sec:ledger}

Every pruning trick that is only conditionally sound is kept out of the
proven core and given a \emph{named hypothesis}: the condition, not the
induction, is the interesting content.  The discipline this buys is a
repository rule: \textbf{a search-changing PR must identify which lemma
it preserves, weakens, or newly depends on} (see
\texttt{formal/README.md} for the full PR-guideline ledger).  Every
unproven assumption is named, stated, and where possible measured.

\texttt{soften\_null\_window} \leanref{soften\_null\_window}{Tricks.lean}
is a small proven lemma: for windows strictly above the mate band,
mate-distance softening does not disturb null-window fail-high
decisions.

\subsection{Shrinking a bet: the layered null-move story}
\label{sec:nullmove}

The ledger is not static; the null-move entry shows a bet being
\emph{shrunk} by restructuring (Figure~\ref{fig:nulllayers}).  Sunfish's
\texttt{can\_null} flag plays four roles at once --- null-move gate,
repetition gate, transposition-key component, and IID recursion flag ---
and \texttt{CanNull.lean} models all four exactly as master runs them
(including two audit surprises recorded there: sunfish permits
\emph{consecutive} null moves, and the move generator's laziness is
semantically load-bearing for the table state).  The old, monolithic
claim ``null-move search satisfies the docstring'' needed a zugzwang
hypothesis in front of everything.  The layered version needs it almost
nowhere:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer 1 (unconditional).}  Define
    $\mathrm{nullValue}$ --- the value function of the
    null-and-repetition-augmented search itself, with the pass option
    (searched at $\mathrm{depth}-3$ behind the gamma-free guard
    $|\mathrm{score}| < 500$) as one more option in the fold.  Then the
    search brackets $\mathrm{nullValue}$ with a \emph{point} spec, and
    the $(\mathrm{pos}, \mathrm{depth}, \mathrm{can\_null})$-keyed table
    stays consistent, with no zugzwang hypothesis anywhere
    \leanref{boundNullTT\_spec, CTableOK}{CanNull.lean}.
    Self-consistency of search plus table is not a bet.
  \item \textbf{Layer 2 (the bet, now smaller).}  Relating
    $\mathrm{nullValue}$ to the null-free value is where the null-move
    bet lives: under \texttt{NullBetOK} (some real move matches the
    reduced-depth pass; the required witness also excludes guard-passing
    stalemates) and an empty history, $\mathrm{nullValue}$ collapses to
    the plain value \leanref{nullValue\_plain}{CanNull.lean}, and
    composing the layers recovers the docstring.
\end{itemize}

Zugzwang --- precisely $\neg\texttt{NullBetOK}$ --- can make the engine
compute the wrong \emph{value}, but can never make its table contradict
its search.  A satisfying corollary of the keyed-table invariant being
history-relative: the empty table satisfies it for \emph{any} history
\leanref{ctableOK\_empty}{CanNull.lean}, which is exactly why sunfish
clears \texttt{tp\_score} whenever the game history changes.

The framing, in one paragraph: the engine is \emph{allowed} to bet ---
that is what selective search is --- but every bet must have a name, a
statement in the ledger, and, where possible, a measured exposure.
Restructuring can shrink a bet (this subsection); a counterexample can
prove a bet is real (Cex.~\ref{cex:stale}); and a discharge can retire
one (Theorem~\ref{thm:futility}).  What is not allowed is an unnamed
bet.

Three narrative points, because the numbers alone mislead:

\paragraph{The price of point-consistency.}
Re-search LMR admits no point spec (Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}), and the
interval-shaped statement we first proved in its place guarantees
nothing (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).  One can buy the point spec back
--- and each way of doing so was priced:
deterministic LMR ($-48 \pm 33$), a conservative reduction condition
($-16 \pm 38$), sticky TT entries ($-33$), ordering-only ($-34$).
Every restoration is ELO-negative.  Likewise, completing null-move
soundness (searching the pass at a depth for which the spec discharges)
was priced at $-28$ and declined: the engine keeps the bet, and the
ledger keeps its name (\texttt{NullBetOK}).  The epilogue is
instructive: knowing both the price \emph{and} what it buys, the
maintainer ultimately chose to pay the smallest one --- master now
ships the conservative deterministic LMR, trading ${\sim}16$ ELO for
point specs end-to-end (Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}).  The decision would
have been incoherent without both loops: the proofs identified exactly
what the 16 ELO purchases, and the tournaments certified it costs no
more than that.

\paragraph{Time-regime artifacts.}
An earlier survey measured reverse futility at $+45$ and mate-score
softening at $+19$.  After a fix to time handling (in-search deadlines
replacing between-iteration checks), both were re-measured on the fixed
baseline: $-1$ and $-22$.  The original numbers were artifacts of the
old time regime, not chess.  Rule: after any time-handling change,
re-baseline everything you believed.

\paragraph{The multi-TC lesson.}
An opening think-time ramp (cap of $0.1\mathrm{s} \times$ ply) shipped
with no cutoff and silently governed \emph{entire games} at long time
controls: at 15+3 the proper budget is ${\sim}25$s/move but the ramp
still bound at move 60, and a user's game ended in mate with 14 of 15
minutes unused.  The bug was invisible at the 4+0.04 test TC, where the
ramp stops binding at ply 2 --- it sailed through 300-game matches.  It
was caught by a user report and fixed the same day; the fix was verified
by driving the per-move time curve directly at 15+3 (17--24s observed
mid-game vs.\ 3--9s before).  This became a standing rule:
time-management changes must be validated at multiple time controls,
including a direct per-move time-curve check, because fast-TC matches
are structurally blind to long-TC time bugs.

\paragraph{Contamination stories, briefly.}
A contaminated run (engines sharing a live checkout) once measured
$-60$ ELO at LOS 0.02\% for a change later shown to search bit-identical
node counts --- hence the freeze rule and the old-vs-old control match.
Tournament managers reuse engine processes across games, so state
surviving \texttt{ucinewgame} acts as invisible cross-game learning: a
four-way eviction-policy round-robin ranked
$+66$ / $+47$ / $+15$ / $-132$ across candidates whose \emph{per-game}
search was in some cases provably identical.  Behavior-neutrality is
checked by depth-limited lockstep (moves \emph{and} node counts), never
by clock.

\subsection{The frame}

Proofs identify the trade; tournaments price it.  Neither loop alone
would have handled LMR correctly: without the proof we would not have
known the crossing existed, that no interval reading rescues it, or
that the killer and stalemate arguments survive reduction; without the
tournaments, the eventual restoration of the point spec
(Section~\ref{sec:lmrdet}) would have been bought blind instead of at
a known price of ${\sim}16$ ELO.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Nipkow's invited ITP 2024 paper~\cite{nipkow2024alphabeta} and the
accompanying Isabelle/HOL AFP entry~\cite{AlphaBetaPruningAFP} verify
fail-hard and fail-soft alpha-beta over linear orders, distributive
lattices and de Morgan domains; the fail-soft correctness statement
there is the two-sided bound that our $\mathrm{BoundSpec}$ specializes
to a null window, and our loop invariant (``the running best is a
fail-soft report of the fold so far'') follows the same shape.
\emph{Formal Verification of Minimax Algorithms}~\cite{dafnyMinimax}
verifies minimax variants with alpha-beta and transposition tables in
Dafny --- the closest prior treatment of the table invariant (our
\texttt{TableOK}).  Knuth and Moore's classical
analysis~\cite{knuth1975alphabeta} and the MTD($f$)
framework~\cite{plaat1996mtdf} supply the algorithmic background;
futility pruning in the form sunfish uses traces to
Heinz~\cite{heinz1998futility}.

Our position: to our knowledge this is the first Lean~4 formalization of
null-window/MTD-bi search, and the first line of work to pair machine
proofs of search soundness with ELO-measured prices on a production
engine --- one that plays rated games in public.  We found no prior
formal treatment of king-capture (pseudo-legal) search, of a stalemate
correction, of killer-table invariants, or of a machine-checked
refutation of transposition-table point-consistency under
gamma-adaptive Late Move Reductions (Cex.~\ref{cex:crossing}).

\section{Lessons and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:lessons}

\paragraph{The model--code gap.}
All theorems are about the Lean model of Section~\ref{sec:model}, not
about \texttt{sunfish.py}.  The gap is managed, not closed: the model is
line-mapped (Figure~\ref{fig:model}; the correspondence table in
\texttt{formal/README.md} pairs every Lean definition with the sunfish
lines it transcribes, audited at commit \texttt{9b1a7b4}), the
counterexamples are constructed to exercise exactly the modeled
mechanism, and features excluded from a given model are excluded
\emph{explicitly} with a stated reason
(Section~\ref{sec:model-not}).  Still, quiescence is collapsed into
\texttt{eval}, move ordering is modeled by its load-bearing consequence
only, and no chess rules are formalized at all.  A reader should trust
the theorems as statements about the mechanisms, quantified over all
abstract games, not as end-to-end verification of the Python.

\paragraph{Two author claims refuted by the proof assistant,
mid-project.}
We record these because they are the strongest evidence in this paper
that the machine was doing work the authors could not do by inspection.
\emph{(1) The fail-high-vs-$V_{\mathrm{full}}$ error.}  While
specifying LMR we claimed that fail-high reports remain sound against
full $\negamax$ --- the re-search guard, we reasoned, ensures no reduced
fail high is ever yielded.  Lean refused the induction: the guard
protects only the \emph{immediately} reduced move, while a parent fail
high is certified by child fail lows, which are only sound against the
children's \emph{reduced} values --- the contamination is recursive and
cannot be scrubbed at one level.  This is why the interval-shaped
statement of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} had to make $\Vhi$'s children
$\Vlo$-valued, and why the mutually recursive pair existed at all ---
before both were deleted as guaranteeing nothing.
\emph{(2) The $V_{\mathrm{reduced}}$ target error.}  We claimed the
folklore fail-low target --- reducible moves simply valued one ply
shallower ($\mathrm{negamaxShallow}$; deleted along with the
interval-shaped statements it fed) --- was the right lower target.
Also false: on the re-search
fall-through, the yielded value bounds the \emph{deep} child value while
the shallow value has just failed high and can exceed everything
yielded; the sound per-move entry is the \emph{minimum} of the two
depths.  Both errors would have survived code review; neither survived
\texttt{lake build}.

\paragraph{Measurement contamination.}
Every rule in \texttt{docs/TESTING.md} exists because its absence
produced a confident wrong number in this repository: the $-60$ ELO
phantom regression (shared checkout), the $\pm$dozens swings from
cross-game table persistence (process reuse), fictional error bars from
deterministic engines without opening books, and the long-TC time bug
invisible at fast TC.  We treat the methodology document as part of the
scientific apparatus, on the same footing as the proofs.

\paragraph{Threats.}
Single engine, single day, one maintainer's conjectures; ELO error bars
at 300 games are $\pm 30$-ish and several middle-block numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:elo} are single runs; the taxonomy of
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} is validated on one engine family
(null-window MTD-bi) and its transfer to PVS/aspiration engines is
argued, not proven.

\section{Availability}
\label{sec:availability}

Everything is in the public sunfish
repository~\cite{sunfish}: the engine (\texttt{sunfish.py}), the Lean
development (\texttt{formal/}, building with \texttt{lake build} from a

is driven.  The engine itself plays continuously on Lichess as
\texttt{sunfish-engine}.
